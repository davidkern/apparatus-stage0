## Description

A text chart is a single document, page, or slide composed solely or mainly of words, used primarily in presentations. Although typically one of a series of charts, each chart tends to stand on its own. Text charts normally contain only the key words or phrases of a subject rather than complete sentences or detailed explanations.

Harris identifies five major variations, each suited to different presentation contexts and content types.

## Examples

### Title Text Chart (p. 400)
> "Typically used as the introductory page or slide in a presentation. In addition to the title, this chart might include such information as purpose of meeting, outline of meeting, key points to be covered, etc."

Example structure:
```
ABC Company
Annual Meeting
- 1996 Results
- 1997 Outlook
- Questions
```

### Bullet Text Chart (p. 400)
> "A text chart where symbols (bullets) highlight the key thoughts or phrases. Dots are most widely used; however, any symbol will work. Different symbols can be used to indicate different types of material or level of indentation."

Example structure:
```
Adult activities
  - Youth activities
  - Table games
  - Races
  - Cookout
```

### Paragraph Text Chart (p. 400)
> "A text chart in which a complete paragraph is used as opposed to just a few key words as in the other types of text charts."

Used for quotations or complete statements requiring full context.

### Column Text Chart (p. 400)
> "A chart where the text is arranged in two or three columns as in a newspaper. Sometimes used for making comparisons, organizing related information, or displaying questions and answers."

Example shows product comparison with attributes in parallel columns.

### Build Text Chart (p. 400)
> "Sometimes referred to as a reveal chart. A build chart is one of a series of charts used to develop/present a broad message, idea, or concept. The first chart of the series would have only the first point on it. The next chart would have points one and two on it."

Harris shows three sequential slides building up a "Sales Strategy" list:
- Chart #1: "More sales calls"
- Chart #2: "More sales calls" + "Bigger incentives"
- Chart #3: "More sales calls" + "Bigger incentives" + "More backup"

## Usage

### When to use each type
- **Title chart**: Opening slide, section dividers, meeting agendas
- **Bullet chart**: Lists of points, features, benefits, action items
- **Paragraph chart**: Quotations, mission statements, policy text
- **Column chart**: Side-by-side comparisons, Q&A format, parallel lists
- **Build chart**: Complex topics requiring progressive revelation, maintaining audience focus

### General guidelines
- Keep content to key words and phrases, not full sentences (except paragraph charts)
- Limit to 5-8 lines per chart
- Limit to 5-8 words per line
- Use graphics sparingly and primarily for decoration
- Ensure each chart can stand alone even in a series

## Notes

Text charts represent the non-data-graphic end of the presentation spectrum. While they contain no quantitative visualization, they serve essential roles in structuring presentations and communicating qualitative information. The build chart variation demonstrates how sequential revelation can control audience attention and build understanding incrementally.
