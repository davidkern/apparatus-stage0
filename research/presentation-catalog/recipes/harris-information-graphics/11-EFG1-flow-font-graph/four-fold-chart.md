## Description

A four-fold chart is a variation of a sector graph when scales are used on the radii, or a variation of a proportional chart when scales are not used. Four-fold charts use four 90-degree segments to represent four different categories. The radius or area of each segment is proportional to the value of the category it represents.

Four-fold charts are often used when four conditions or situations are generated by two variables. This makes them particularly suited to experimental designs or comparative studies with two binary factors.

## Examples

> "For example, if a weight loss study is done where there were two distinct variations of diet and exercise, the following four categories of participants would be generated: those participants on a strenuous exercise program with and without a special diet and those on a mild exercise program with and without a special diet. Plotting the results of this type of study on a four-fold chart can sometimes make the results stand out clearer." (p. 159)

Harris illustrates a four-fold graph showing weight loss results (p. 159):
- Quadrants labeled: "Strenuous exercise & diet", "Strenuous exercise & no diet", "Diet & mild exercise", "No diet & mild exercise"
- A scale showing "10 15 20 25 Lbs. lost"
- Sectors of varying sizes showing relative weight loss in each condition

## Usage

Four-fold charts are appropriate when:
- Data naturally falls into four categories from two binary variables
- You want to show relative magnitudes across the four conditions
- The 2x2 structure of the data should be visually apparent

Unless the chart is only meant to approximate values, the numeric values are frequently shown on or adjacent to the data graphics. This allows viewers to read precise values while still seeing the visual comparison.

## Notes

The four-fold chart makes the interaction between two variables visually apparent. In the weight loss example, viewers can quickly see whether the combination of diet and strenuous exercise produces notably different results than either factor alone.
