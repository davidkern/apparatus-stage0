## Description

A graph is a chart that graphically displays quantitative relationships between two or more groups of information - for example, the relationship between cities and their populations, a car's speed and its efficiency, or the buying power of a dollar over time.

> "Graphs have combinations of one, two, or three straight or circular axes utilizing one or more quantitative scales. This definition clearly differentiates graphs from other charts such as diagrams, tables, text charts, proportional charts, illustration charts, and most maps." (p. 164)

Since graphs constitute one of the major categories of charts, graphs are frequently referred to as charts.

## Benefits of Graphs

Graphs offer many important features:
- Large amounts of information can be conveniently and effectively reviewed
- Overall patterns of data stand out more clearly than in tabulated form
- Deviations, trends, and relationships many times are more noticeable
- Comparisons and projections can many times be made more easily and accurately
- Anomalies in data frequently become obvious
- Viewers can more rapidly determine and absorb the essence of the information
- When used in presentations, graphs shorten meetings and expedite group decisions

## Categorization Methods

### By Major Family or Configuration

Most graphs can be categorized into one or more families (p. 164):
- **Point graph**: Scatter plots, data points
- **Line graph**: Connected data points showing trends
- **Area graph**: Filled regions below lines
- **Column graph**: Vertical bars
- **Bar graph**: Horizontal bars
- **Circular graphs**: Polar graph, circle graph, circular line graph
- **Vector graph**: Arrows showing magnitude and direction
- **Radar graph**: Multiple axes radiating from center
- **Nomograph**: Alignment charts for calculation
- **Contour graph**: Elevation/value lines on 2D surface
- **Wireframe graph**: 3D mesh surfaces
- **Surface graph**: Filled 3D surfaces
- **Trilinear graph**: Triangular coordinates

Within each family there are major subcategories. For instance, in the column graph family: simple, grouped, overlapped, stacked, floating, range, difference, deviation, pictorial, and progressive column graphs.

### By Area or Field of Use

Three primary usage categories (p. 165):

**Technical/scientific graphs:**
- Often very specialized with higher percentage of 3D graphs
- Large amounts of data common
- Data frequently entered via equations or real-time collection
- Examples: polar, wireframe, contour, vector, surface graphs

**Data analysis graphs (statistical):**
- Determining meaningful relationships, data distribution, significance
- Examples: quantile, probability, scatter, histogram, box graphs

**Business graphs:**
- Analyzing and planning: index, bubble, supply/demand, spider, Pareto
- Monitoring and controlling: vertical line, deviation, moving average, control, high-low-close
- Presentation: emphasis on aesthetics, ease of understanding, focused message

### By Type of Data Plotted

**Original data** (p. 166):
- Data in unmodified form
- Empirical observations: people counts, temperature, pressure, sales
- The majority of graphs use this type

**Derived data:**
- Generated by mathematically manipulating original data
- Examples: index, ratio, deviation, residual, difference, change, relative value
- Cannot be observed directly but often more meaningful (e.g., crimes per thousand inhabitants)

**Abstract data:**
- Illustrates concepts, principles, theories, or generalities
- Example: break-even graph showing cost/volume/profit relationships
- Minimal or no axis labels; some call these charts or diagrams

### By Number of Axes

| Geometry | One Axis | Two Axes | Three Axes |
|----------|----------|----------|------------|
| Rectangular | Point graph on single scale | Scatter graph | 3D scatter/surface |
| Circular | Circle/pie graph | Polar graph | Polar with radius variable |
| Triangular | N/A | N/A | Trilinear graph |

### By Purpose

**Analyzing, exploring, planning** (p. 167):
- Focus on extracting information for observations, conclusions, plans
- Almost every graph form used except presentation-specific types
- Techniques like curve fitting, tolerance intervals, 3D rotation used more frequently
- No limit on data amount as long as legible
- Content emphasized over appearance

**Monitoring and controlling:**
- Observing values over time to determine if action needed
- Sequence graphs, particularly time series
- Line and column most common; point and area occasionally
- Reference values (budget, plan, goal) often included
- Deviation graphs showing difference from reference

**Presentation:**
- Communicating specific information between people/groups
- Can contain large data for small groups; reduced for large audiences with slides
- Techniques like pictures, perspective, graduated fills used more
- Equal emphasis on content and appearance
- Color used extensively

**Publications, reports, reference documents:**
- Range from simple newspaper graphs to complex technical publications
- Focus, types, enhancements vary with application
- Equal emphasis on content and appearance
