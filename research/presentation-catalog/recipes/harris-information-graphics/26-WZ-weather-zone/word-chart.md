## Description

A word chart is a single document, page, slide, etc., consisting solely or mainly of words and used primarily in presentations. Although a word chart is generally one of a series of charts, each chart tends to stand on its own.

Word charts normally contain only the key words or phrases of a subject, serving as visual support for oral presentation rather than complete documentation.

## Examples

> "Sometimes referred to as a text chart. A word chart is a single document, page, slide, etc., consisting solely or mainly of words and used primarily in presentations. Although a word chart is generally one of a series of charts, each chart tends to stand on its own. Word charts normally contain only the key words or phrases of a subject. Typical guidelines recommend that the number of lines per chart and the number of words per line be limited to between five and eight." (p. 441)

Harris illustrates two examples:
1. "Improved benefits" with bullet points: "More life insurance", "Bonus program", "401-K improvements"
2. "Improved Features" with bullet points: "Speed", "Memory", "Price" (accompanied by a small graphic)

### Graphics Usage
> "Most word charts have no graphics associated with them except for decorative purposes or general orientation." (p. 441)

## Usage

Word charts are appropriate for:
- Presentation slides
- Meeting visual aids
- Training materials
- Executive summaries

### Design Guidelines

**Content limits**:
- 5-8 lines per chart
- 5-8 words per line
- Key words only, not complete sentences

**Structure**:
- Clear title identifying the topic
- Bulleted or numbered key points
- Consistent formatting throughout a series

**Graphics**:
- Most word charts have no graphics
- When used, graphics serve decorative or orientation purposes
- Should not distract from the text content

### Presentation Context

Word charts work best when:
- Speaker provides elaboration verbally
- Audience can see key points at a glance
- Each chart makes a single coherent point
- The series maintains visual consistency

## Notes

The 5-8 guideline for both lines and words reflects cognitive limitations - audiences can absorb only so much text at once. Word charts that exceed these limits often result in the audience reading ahead rather than listening to the speaker.
