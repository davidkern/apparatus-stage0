## Description

The sixteenth common topic argues from proportional or analogous results (ek tou analogon symbainein). If a certain treatment follows from a certain condition, then a proportionally different treatment should follow from a proportionally different condition.

The structure is: As A is to B, so C should be to D. If we treat condition A one way, we should treat condition C in a proportionally corresponding way.

## Examples

> "Another is from consequences by analogy. For example, when they tried to force his son who was underage to perform public services because he was tall, Iphicrates said that if they deem large boys men, they should vote that small men are boys." (1399b2-6)

The city wanted Iphicrates' tall but underage son to perform civic duties reserved for adults. Iphicrates exposes the absurdity by completing the analogy:
- If tall boys → treated as men
- Then small men → should be treated as boys

The reductio ad absurdum shows the original reasoning is flawed: size should not determine civic status.

> "And Theodectes in the Law [speech said], 'Since you are making citizens of mercenary soldiers, for example, Strabax and Charidemus, because of their merits, will you not make exiles of those among the mercenaries who have wrought irreparable damage?'" (1399b6-12)

The argument:
- Meritorious mercenaries → granted citizenship (positive treatment for positive contribution)
- Harmful mercenaries → should be exiled (proportionally negative treatment for negative contribution)

If merit earns citizenship, then harm should earn exile. The proportional structure demands consistent treatment.

## Usage

Use this topic when:
- An inconsistent treatment is being proposed or has occurred
- A proportional relationship can be established
- Completing the proportion exposes inconsistency or establishes your conclusion

The technique works particularly well for:
- **Exposing double standards**: If A gets treatment X, then B (analogous to A) should get treatment proportional to X
- **Demanding consistency**: Completing the proportion shows what consistency requires
- **Reductio arguments**: Showing that a principle, if consistently applied, leads to absurdity

## Notes

This topic is related to analogy (Topic 4a) but specifically emphasizes proportionality: not just that like cases should be treated alike, but that different cases should be treated proportionally differently.

The Iphicrates example shows rhetorical wit: the argument is memorable because the completed analogy is obviously absurd, which reflects back on the original proposal.
