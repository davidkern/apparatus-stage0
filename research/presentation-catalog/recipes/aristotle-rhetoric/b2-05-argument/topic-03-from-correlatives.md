## Description

The third common topic argues from correlative relationships (pros allēla - "toward one another"). If something is predicated honorably or justly of one party in a relationship, it should be predicated of the corresponding party. If it is shameful to sell, it is not shameful to buy; if one party rightly commands, the other party rightly obeys.

However, Aristotle warns extensively about the risk of false reasoning with this topic. The correlation is not automatic - one must examine both whether the action was deserved and whether the specific agent was the right one to perform it.

## Examples

> "If to have done honorably or justly is predicated of one of a pair, to have experienced [it honestly or justly] belongs to the other, and if [one person has the right] to give an order, [the other has the right] also to act." (1397a27-30)

The basic structure: correlative pairs share predicates.

> "What Diomedon the tax farmer said about the taxes: 'If it is not shameful for you to sell them, neither is it for me to buy.'" (1397a31-33)

Diomedon argues that if the city can honorably sell tax-collection rights, he can honorably buy them. The correlation transfers the moral predicate.

> "In the Alcmeon of Theodectes: [Alphesiboa asks,] 'Did no one of mortals loathe your mother?' In reply, [Alcmeon] says, 'But one should examine the statement by taking it apart.' And when Alphesiboa asks how, he takes it up and says, 'They judged she should die, but not that I should kill her.'" (1397b1-6)

This example shows the **failure** of simple correlative reasoning. The mother deserved to die (one side of the correlation), but Alcmeon was not the right agent to kill her (the correlation fails on the agent side). The predicates do not transfer automatically.

> "The case of the man murdered at Thebes, about whom [the defendant] demands a judgment as to whether he justly deserved to die, on the ground that it was not unjust to kill someone who died justly." (1397b9-11)

Similar structure: if the victim deserved death, then killing him was not unjust.

## Usage

Use this topic when:
- Two parties stand in a clear correlative relationship
- A predicate has been established for one party
- You want to argue the predicate transfers to the other

Critical precaution:
> "One ought separately to look at whether the sufferer deserves to suffer and whether the agent of the suffering is the right person to have acted, then to use whichever argument fits." (1397b1-5)

The topic has two components that must both be examined:
1. Does the patient/receiver deserve what they received?
2. Is the agent the appropriate one to have acted?

## Notes

This topic's extensive warning section is notable. Aristotle takes pains to show both valid uses and the specific way the reasoning can fail. The Alcmeon example demonstrates that correlative relationships can be "taken apart" - disaggregated into separate judgments that don't necessarily align.
