## Description

The eighteenth common topic argues from changed or reversed choices (ek tou anapalin haireisthai - "from choosing the reverse"). The speaker shows that someone's preferences before an event contradict their preferences after, exposing inconsistency.

The structure reveals that the same person values conflicting things depending on circumstances, suggesting incoherence or bad faith.

## Examples

> "Another is from not always choosing the same thing before or after [an event], but the reverse; for example, this enthymeme: '[It would be terrible] if when in exile we fought to come home, but having come home we shall go into exile in order not to fight.'" (1399b25-30)

The argument exposes a reversal:
- In exile: chose to fight in order to return home
- At home: would choose exile in order to avoid fighting

The inconsistency: fighting was acceptable to gain home, but home is abandoned to avoid fighting. This suggests the values are not coherent.

> "Sometimes people have chosen to be at home at the cost of fighting, sometimes not to fight at the cost of not remaining at home." (1399b30-1400a1)

Aristotle makes explicit the contradiction: the same people at different times make opposite trade-offs between the same values (home vs. fighting).

Kennedy notes this is "apparently an adaptation of Lysias 34.11 on the situation in Athens in 403 BCE."

## Usage

Use this topic when:
- Someone's choices at different times contradict each other
- The inconsistency reveals problematic reasoning or values
- You want to demand consistency or expose bad faith

The argument is particularly effective for:
- **Political consistency**: Showing a group's positions have shifted opportunistically
- **Personal credibility**: Showing someone's values change based on convenience
- **Demanding coherence**: Using past choices to constrain present options

The structure can also work defensively: showing your choices have been consistent over time strengthens credibility.

## Notes

The historical context (Athens in 403 BCE) involves the return of democratic exiles after the rule of the Thirty Tyrants. The argument apparently addresses those who fought to return but then want to avoid further conflict.

This topic is related to Topic 22 (from contradictions) but specifically focuses on temporal contradiction in choices rather than logical contradiction in statements.
