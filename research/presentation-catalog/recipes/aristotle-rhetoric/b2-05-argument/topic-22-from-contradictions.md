## Description

The twenty-second common topic is refutative, examining contradictions (ta anomologoumena - "things not in agreement"). The speaker looks for inconsistencies that undermine credibility or prove a claim false.

Aristotle identifies three targets for contradiction analysis:
1. Against the opponent only
2. Against the speaker (to establish their own consistency)
3. Against both (comparative credibility)

## Examples

> "Another is refutative, a matter of looking at contradictions [in three ways]: once as applies only to the opponent (if something is contradicted by all dates, actions, and words)." (1400b3-5)

The first application: Find contradictions in the opponent's statements, timing, or actions.

> "For example, 'And he says he loves you, but he took the oath with the Thirty.'" (1400b5-7)

The opponent claims to love the Athenian people but was allied with the Thirty Tyrants who oppressed them. The claim contradicts the action.

> "Once as applicable to the speaker: 'And he says I am litigious, but he cannot show that I have brought any case to be judged in court.'" (1400b7-9)

The second application: The speaker shows their own record contradicts the opponent's accusation. Being called "litigious" is contradicted by having no court cases.

> "And once as applicable to the speaker and the opponent: 'And he has never lent any money, but I have even ransomed many of you.'" (1400b9-11)

The third application: Comparative credibility. The opponent's stinginess contradicts the speaker's generosity to the community.

## Usage

Use this topic when:
- The opponent has made statements or taken actions that conflict
- You can demonstrate your own consistency in contrast
- Comparative credibility can be established through contrasting records

Three modes of application:

**Against opponent only:**
- Examine their dates, actions, and words for internal conflicts
- Show that what they say contradicts what they've done

**For the speaker (defensive):**
- Show that accusations against you contradict the evidence
- Demonstrate your consistency across time

**Comparative:**
- Contrast opponent's record with your own
- Establish credibility through demonstrated virtue vs. demonstrated vice

## Notes

The reference to "the Thirty" places this in Athenian political context: the oligarchic regime of 404 BCE that was deeply hated. Alliance with the Thirty was a powerful accusation that contradicted any claim of democratic loyalty.

This topic is closely related to Topic 18 (changed choices) but broader: it examines any contradiction, not just temporal inconsistency in preferences.
