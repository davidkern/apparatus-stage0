## Description

The twenty-sixth common topic examines contraries together (hamaskopein - "to look at together"). When a new action is proposed that contradicts a previous action, the speaker places them side by side to expose the inconsistency.

The technique reveals that one cannot consistently do both: if the previous action was right, the new action is wrong, and vice versa.

## Examples

> "Another, when something is about to be done that is contrary to what has been done, is to look at them together." (1401a4-6)

Aristotle states the method: examine proposed and past actions together.

> "For example, when the people of Elea asked Xenophanes if they should sacrifice and sing dirges to Leucothea or not, he advised them not to sing dirges if they regarded her as a god, and if as a human being not to sacrifice." (1401a6-12)

The Eleans faced a decision about honoring Leucothea (a figure who was human but became a goddess). Xenophanes exposed the inconsistency:

- If she is a god → sacrifice is appropriate, but dirges (mourning songs) are not
- If she is a human → dirges are appropriate, but sacrifice is not

You cannot do both - they imply contradictory views of her nature. Xenophanes forces them to choose a consistent position.

Kennedy notes: "For the example to be apposite the Eleans must earlier have either sacrificed to her or sung dirges" - the advice exposes that their past practice was inconsistent with any coherent view.

## Usage

Use this topic when:
- A new action is proposed that conflicts with past practice
- The inconsistency can be made explicit
- Forcing a choice between the two positions serves your argument

The technique is particularly effective for:
- **Religious/ritual matters**: Where symbolic actions should cohere
- **Political decisions**: Where policy should be consistent
- **Ethical judgments**: Where principles should apply uniformly

The Xenophanes example shows philosophical application: using logical consistency to force clarification of underlying beliefs.

## Notes

Xenophanes of Colophon (6th century BCE) was a philosopher-poet known for criticizing conventional religious beliefs. His advice about Leucothea exemplifies his method: using logical consistency to expose problems in traditional practice.

The topic shares structure with Topic 1 (from opposites) but applies specifically to temporal contradiction: past action vs. proposed future action.
