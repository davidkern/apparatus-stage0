## Description

The seventh type of apparent enthymeme takes a non-cause as a cause (para to anaition). When something happens after something else, people assume the earlier event caused the later. This is the classic post hoc ergo propter hoc fallacy: "after this, therefore because of this."

Aristotle notes this fallacy is especially common among "people involved in politics."

## Examples

> "Another is taking a non-cause as a cause; for example, when something has happened at the same time or after [something else]; for people take what happens later as though it happened because of what proceeded." (1402a2-5)

Aristotle states the principle: temporal sequence is mistaken for causal connection.

> "And especially people involved in politics, for example, the way Demades [regarded] the policy of Demosthenes as the cause of all evils; for the war took place after it." (1402a5-8)

The political example:
- Demosthenes advocated a certain policy
- War followed
- Demades blamed Demosthenes' policy for causing the war

But temporal sequence doesn't establish causation. The war might have happened regardless, or might have had different causes entirely.

Kennedy notes: "This is the only certain reference to the orator Demosthenes in the Rhetoric and one of the latest historical references. 'The war' is that between Athens and its allies and Macedon, culminating in the defeat of the Greeks at Chaeronea in 338 BCE."

## Usage

**For recognition:**
- Notice when "X happened, then Y happened" is presented as "X caused Y"
- Ask: "What other causes might explain Y?"
- Ask: "Would Y have happened even without X?"

**Political applications (where the fallacy is especially common):**
- Blaming the party in power for all ills that follow
- Crediting the party in power for all goods that follow
- Attributing complex outcomes to single policy decisions

**Key distinction:**
- Temporal sequence: X before Y (observation)
- Causal connection: X because of Y (requires additional evidence)

## Notes

Aristotle's choice of a political example is telling - he observes that this fallacy is "especially" common in political discourse. The complexity of political causation makes it easy to attribute outcomes to salient preceding events, regardless of actual causal connection.

Demades was an orator who often opposed Demosthenes. His attribution of "all evils" to Demosthenes' policy exemplifies political scapegoating through false causation.
