## Description

Aristotle observes that audiences prefer refutative enthymemes over demonstrative ones. This is not a topic for constructing arguments but a psychological insight about argument reception.

Refutative enthymemes bring together opposites in brief form, making them immediately clear when set side by side. Audiences applaud them more readily.

## Examples

> "Refutative enthymemes are better liked [by audiences] than demonstrative ones because the refutative enthymeme is a bringing together of opposites in brief form, and when these are set side by side they are clearer to the hearer." (1400b25-29)

The reason for preference: refutation creates a clear contrast. Opposites placed together illuminate each other.

> "In the case of all syllogistic argument, both refutative and demonstrative, those are most applauded that [hearers] foresee from the beginning, but not because they are superficial (at the same time, too, people are pleased with themselves when anticipating [the conclusion])." (1400b29-33)

Two conditions for applause:
1. Hearers can foresee the conclusion from the beginning
2. But the argument is not superficial

The pleasure comes partly from successful anticipation - audiences enjoy the satisfaction of seeing where an argument leads.

> "And [they like] those they are slower to apprehend to the extent that they understand when these have been stated." (1400b33-34)

Even arguments that take time to understand please audiences - as long as understanding comes when the conclusion is stated. There's pleasure in the moment of comprehension.

## Usage

This observation guides rhetorical presentation:

**For maximum impact:**
- Structure arguments as refutations when possible
- Bring contrasts together briefly and clearly
- Allow audiences to anticipate conclusions (but don't be simplistic)
- Ensure the conclusion is graspable when stated

**Psychological mechanism:**
- Audiences enjoy the clarity of opposition
- They take pleasure in successful anticipation
- They appreciate the "aha" moment of comprehension

This explains why many of the 28 topics work through contrast, opposition, or comparison - these structures generate the clarity audiences prefer.

## Notes

This passage provides Aristotle's explanation for WHY certain rhetorical structures work better than others. The preference for refutation reflects a cognitive tendency: contrast makes things clearer than isolated demonstration.

The qualification about superficiality is important: audiences want to feel clever for anticipating the conclusion, but they don't want the argument to be too easy. The ideal is accessible depth.
