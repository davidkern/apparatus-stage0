## Description

The seventeenth common topic argues from identical results (to symbainon t'auton - "the result is the same"). If two different things or actions lead to the same result, they can be treated equivalently in evaluation.

The structure: A and B both result in C. Therefore A and B can be judged by reference to C, and what is said of one can be said of the other.

Aristotle notes this topic is flexible: "One should take up whichever argument is useful."

## Examples

> "Another is from [arguing] that if some result is the same, the things from which it resulted are also. For example, Xenophanes said that those who say that the gods are born are as impious as those who say that they die; for in both cases, the result is that at some time the gods do not exist." (1399b12-17)

Xenophanes argues:
- Saying gods are born → implies gods once didn't exist
- Saying gods die → implies gods at some point won't exist
- Same result: divine non-existence at some time
- Therefore: both claims are equally impious

> "And in general [this topic is a matter of] taking the result of each thing as always the same." (1399b17-18)

Aristotle generalizes the principle.

> "[For example,] 'You are going to make a judgment not about Isocrates, but about education: whether it is right to study philosophy.'" (1399b18-20)

Reframing a trial: the result of convicting Isocrates is the same as condemning education/philosophy. The specific case is connected to a general principle via shared result.

> "And that to give 'earth and water' is to be a slave, and that to share in the 'common peace' is to do what is commanded." (1399b20-22)

Political applications:
- Giving earth and water (symbolic submission to Persia) → slavery
- Accepting the "common peace" (imposed by Macedon) → doing what is commanded

The symbolic act and its result are equated.

> "One should take up whichever argument is useful." (1399b22-23)

Aristotle explicitly notes the rhetorical flexibility: this topic can argue for or against treating things as equivalent.

## Usage

Use this topic when:
- Two different actions or claims lead to the same outcome
- You want to transfer evaluation from one to the other
- The shared result is significant and recognized

Applications include:
- **Equivalence arguments**: Showing two things deserve the same judgment
- **Reframing**: Connecting a specific case to a broader principle
- **Exposure**: Showing the real meaning of an act by identifying its result

The Xenophanes example demonstrates philosophical application; the political examples show practical rhetorical use.

## Notes

Kennedy notes the "common peace" reference may be "the latest datable reference in the Rhetoric" - the peace forced on Greek cities by Macedon after 338 BCE.

The topic connects to Aristotle's interest in causation: things that produce identical effects can, for rhetorical purposes, be treated as equivalent causes.
