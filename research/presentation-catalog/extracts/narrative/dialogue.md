**Definition**: Representing spoken exchange between characters.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants the audience to experience direct
interaction—what characters say to each other, revealing relationship,
conflict, and personality through speech.

**Sub-intents**:

**Direct Dialogue**: Exact words in quotation.
- "The database is down," she said.
- Creates immediacy and character voice.

**Indirect Dialogue**: Summary of what was said.
- She informed the team that the database had failed.
- Creates narrative flow and compression.

**Free Indirect Discourse**: Character voice blended with narrator.
- The database was down. Great. Just what she needed.
- Creates intimacy without quotation marks.

**Containment**:
- Contains: Statement, Question, Command (within dialogue)
- Contained by: Scene

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Distinct from Quotation (which
attributes source). Dialogue is dramatic and character-building.
