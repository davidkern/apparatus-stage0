**Name:** Principle-with-Humility
**Definition:** Apply design principles thoughtfully rather than rigidly, recognizing that word authority can distort vision.

**How Tufte articulates it:**
> "The principles should not be applied rigidly or in a peevish spirit; they are not logically or mathematically certain; and it is better to violate any principle than to place graceless or inelegant marks on paper."

> "Most principles of design should be greeted with some skepticism, for word authority can dominate our vision, and we may come to see only through the lenses of word authority rather than with our own eyes."

**Examples referenced:**
- None specific

**Framework category:** Meta-intent (guidance on applying other intents)

**Source type:** Explicit prose (epilogue)

**Notes:** This is a second-order intent about how to apply all other principles. It warns against dogmatism and emphasizes judgment over rule-following.
