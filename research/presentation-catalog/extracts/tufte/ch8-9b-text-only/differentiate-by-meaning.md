**Name:** Weight-by-Importance
**Definition:** Use visual weight (line thickness, etc.) to represent semantic importance—give greater visual weight to data measures, lesser to structural elements.

**How Tufte articulates it:**
> "The contrast in line weight represents contrast in meaning. The greater meaning is given to the greater line weight; thus the data line should receive greater weight than the connecting verticals."

> "The logic here is a restatement, in different language, of the principle of data-ink maximization."

**Examples referenced:**
- Time-series example showing data line with greater weight than grid
- Diller painting with perpendicular lines of differing weights

**Framework category:** Emphasis

**Source type:** Explicit prose

**Notes:** Maps visual encoding principles to semantic hierarchy.
