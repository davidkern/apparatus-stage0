**Name:** Favor-Horizontal
**Definition:** Design graphics wider than tall to leverage natural eye movement and facilitate labeling.

**How Tufte articulates it:**
Several justifications provided:
1. "Analogy to the horizon. Our eye is naturally practiced in detecting deviations from the horizon."
2. "Ease of labeling. It is easier to write and to read words that read from left to right on a horizontally stretched plotting field."
3. "Emphasis on causal influence... a longer horizontal helps to elaborate the workings of the causal variable in more detail."
4. Tukey's counsel: "Such plots are rather more often better made wider than tall. Wider-than-tall shapes usually make it easier for the eye to follow from left to right."
5. "Playfair's example. Of the 89 graphics in six different books by William Playfair, most (92 percent) are wider than tall."

**Examples referenced:**
- Plot showing dimensions of all 89 Playfair graphics
- Golden Rectangle discussion (1.618 ratio)
- Playfair's preferred proportions (1.4 to 1.8)

**Framework category:** Temporal (for time-series) / compositional principle

**Source type:** Explicit prose with empirical support

**Notes:** The recommendation is "move toward horizontal graphics about 50 percent wider than tall" unless data suggests otherwise. Exception noted: "smoothly-changing curves can stand being taller than wide, but a wiggly curve needs to be wider than tall."
