**Name:** Natural Visual Ordering

**Definition:** The purpose of using visual encodings that have inherent perceptual hierarchy, allowing viewers to comprehend magnitude and sequence without learned conventions.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "Color often generates graphical puzzles. Despite our experiences with the spectrum in science textbooks and rainbows, the mind's eye does not readily give a visual ordering to colors." In contrast: "Because they do have a natural visual hierarchy, varying shades of gray show varying quantities better than color." (p. 154)

**Specific examples:**
- The "mountain-to-the-sea method" by Vauthier (white for snow/maximum, green for forests, yellow for plains, blue for sea/minimum) is described as "ingenious in idea but not very satisfactory in practice"
- Ten gray shades in the galaxies map "worked effectively"
- Red can reflect "higher levels than other colors, as in the hot spots of the cancer map"

**Framework category:** **Distributional** - encoding magnitude and spread through naturally ordered visual variables
