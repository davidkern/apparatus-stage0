**Name:** Anti-Sparsity

**Definition:** The purpose of avoiding graphics that waste visual space relative to information content, representing a failure to respect the viewer's perceptual capacity.

**How Tufte articulates it:** By calculating data densities that reveal underperformance: "this overwrought display (originally printed in five colors) presents a data matrix of four entries... The graph covers 26.5 square inches... resulting in a data density of .15 numbers per square inch... which is thin indeed." (p. 162)

**Specific examples:**
- The participation rate bar chart: 4 data entries in 26.5 square inches = 0.15 numbers/sq inch
- JASA style sheet example: "3.8 numbers per square inch" for 32 entries
- Contrast with New York weather history: "181 numbers per square inch"
- The extreme example uses five colors and 26.5 square inches for information that could fit in a sentence

**Framework category:** **Aggregative** - efficient use of display resources
