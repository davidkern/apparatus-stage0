1. **Geographic Pattern Revelation** - The population density map reveals landform influences (Appalachian ridges, California's Central Valley, river corridors) that Tufte mentions but doesn't theorize as a distinct communicative intent. 2. **Urban Corridor Communication** - The population density map shows "corridors connecting the major urban complexes" - a specific spatial relationship type (linear urban agglomeration) that emerges visually but isn't named as an intent category. This represents communicating linear connectivity distinct from point clusters.

3. **Proportional Label Spacing** - In the slope graph, country names are spaced vertically in proportion to their percentage values, not equally spaced. This embeds quantitative encoding in typography itself, using whitespace as data carrier. Mentioned but not theorized as a technique.

4. **Crossing-Line Salience** - In the slope graph, when lines cross (indicating rank reversal), the crossing creates visual salience not discussed in text. This is a discoverable pattern-detection mechanism inherent in the form.

6. **Night-Sky Metaphor** - The population density map (dark background, white dots for population) visually evokes satellite imagery of city lights at night. This metaphorical resonance aids comprehension but isn't discussed - the encoding maps naturally to familiar imagery.
