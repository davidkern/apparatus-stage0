**Name:** Data Integration with Separation

**Definition:** The purpose of showing information that is both connected through related content and separated through distinct visual paths, allowing multiple independent readings of the same graphic.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "The information shown is both integrated and separated: integrated through its connected content, separated in that the eye follows several different and uncluttered paths in looking over the data." (p. 159)

**Specific examples:**
- The table-graphic showing government tax collections (1970 vs 1979) for 15 countries
- Vertical reading: ranks countries by percentage, names spaced proportionally
- Horizontal reading: paired comparisons show change over years
- Diagonal reading: slopes compared across all lines, unusual slopes "stand out from the overall upward pattern"
- The arrow diagram shows three independent reading directions

**Framework category:** **Comparative** - enables multiple comparison modes within single display
