**Name:** Multiple Viewing Depths

**Definition:** The purpose of designing graphics with multiple layers of information accessible at different scales of attention: overview from distance, detail up close, and implicit underlying structure.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "Graphics can be designed to have at least three viewing depths: (1) what is seen from a distance, an overall structure usually aggregated from an underlying microstructure; (2) what is seen up close and in detail, the fine structure of the data; and (3) what is seen implicitly, underlying the graphic--that which is behind the graphic." (p. 155)

**Specific examples:**
- The Census Bureau population density map where "each dot, except in urban centers, represents 500 people"
- The map reveals at different depths: urban corridors, landform effects (California's central valley, Appalachian valleys and ridges), river clusters, highway town strings
- "The map arrays, in effect, some 400,000 points on its implicit grid"

**Framework category:** **Aggregative** - enables overview-to-detail navigation of summarized information
