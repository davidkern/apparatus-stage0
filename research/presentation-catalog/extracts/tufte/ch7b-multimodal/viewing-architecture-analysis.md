**Name:** Viewing Architecture Analysis

**Definition:** The purpose of systematically analyzing and designing the visual paths through which a viewer will navigate a graphic.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "Such an analysis of the viewing architecture of a graphic will help in creating and evaluating designs that organize complex information hierarchically." (p. 159)

**Specific examples:**
- The arrow diagram showing three reading directions for the table-graphic
- Decomposition of Ayres' divisions display into three visual angles
- Analysis of slope graph into vertical, horizontal, and comparative readings

**Framework category:** **Instructional** - a methodological intent about design evaluation

---

## Summary: Core Principles from This Section

This section establishes principles around two major themes:

**1. Anti-Puzzle Design:** Graphics should enable visual rather than verbal processing
- Natural visual ordering (gray over arbitrary color)
- Multiple viewing depths (overview, detail, implicit)
- Multiple visual angles (independent reading paths)
- Information flow organization

**2. Data Density:** Graphics should respect human perceptual capability
- High resolution capacity of the eye
- Data density as quality metric
- Anti-sparsity as design goal
- Condensation as aesthetic achievement
