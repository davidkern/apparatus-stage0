**Name:** Data Density Maximization

**Definition:** The purpose of using the eye's high resolution capability to present maximum information per unit area, respecting rather than underestimating visual perception capacity.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "Our eyes can make a remarkable number of distinctions within a small area." He provides the data density formula: "data density of a graphic = number of entries in data matrix / area of data graphic" (p. 161-162)

**Specific examples:**
- 625 points distinguishable per square inch using light grid lines
- 80 x 80 grid creates "25,281 distinctions" in one square inch
- Statistical Abstract typography: 185 characters per square inch actual density
- Maps with "distinctions at 0.1 mm mean 254 per inch"
- Cartographer quote: "conciseness is of the essence and high resolution graphics are a common denominator of cartography"

**Framework category:** **Aggregative** - efficient communication of summary information
