These intents were demonstrated through examining the figures:

1. **Distributional Summary Display** - The erased parallel schematic plot (p. 125) visually shows how minimal vertical lines plus median dots convey full five-number summaries; comparing original boxed version to erased version shows information preservation
2. **Baseline Redundancy Recognition** - Bar chart sequence (pp. 127-128) shows progressive removal until bars float without baseline, visually demonstrating the bars self-establish their origin
3. **White Grid Coordinate Display** - Bar chart with white stripes cutting through (p. 128) shows the visual mechanism clearer than prose description
4. **Range-frame evolution** - Side-by-side comparison (p. 131) of conventional scatterplot vs. range-frame visually demonstrates how frame trimming adds information
5. **Quartile plot** - Visual (p. 132) shows additional tick marks at quartile positions creating five-number summary in margins
6. **Dot-dash-plot** - Complex visual (p. 133) shows marginal dashes framing central scatter - relationship between density of dashes and data distribution visible
7. **Rugplot** - Cascading connected scatterplots (p. 135) show how marginal dashes align across plots
8. **Time series with historical frame** - Chart (p. 132) shows frame lines marking historical high/low, current data position relative to historical bounds visible
9. **Stem-and-leaf plot** - Volcano heights (p. 140) show digits forming histogram shape
10. **Living histogram** - Photograph shows actual humans arranged by height forming bimodal distribution
11. **Ayres division chart** - Division numbers stacked through time (p. 141) show cumulative structure and individual tracking possible
