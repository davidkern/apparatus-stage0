**Name:** Multi-Plot Continuity

**Definition:** The purpose of connecting multiple bivariate displays so the viewer can trace how a single observation transforms through a series of variable relationships.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "The fringe of dashes in the dot-dash-plot can connect a series of bivariate scatters in a rugplot... the dashes encourage the eye to notice how each plot filters and translates the data through the scatter from one adjacent plot to the next." (p. 135)

**Specific examples:**
- Rugplot design: "the quantitative history of a single observation can be traced through a series of one- and two-dimensional contexts" (p. 135)
- Variable sequence can "wander off as appropriate" rather than following fixed order
- Visual shows cascading scatterplots with aligned marginal distributions

**Framework category:** **Relational** - traces transformation relationships across variable chains
