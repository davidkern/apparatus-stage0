**Name:** Self-Explanation without Legend

**Definition:** The purpose of designing graphics that communicate their encoding scheme without requiring external legends, keys, or decoding instructions.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "A range-frame does not require any viewing or decoding instructions; it is not a graphical puzzle and most viewers can easily tell what is going on." (p. 131)

**Specific examples:**
- Range-frame: frame position self-evidently marks data extent
- Dot-dash-plot is "more difficult, although it still shows all the standard information found in the scatterplot" (p. 136)
- "Nothing is lost to those puzzled by the frame of dashes, and something is gained by those who do understand"

**Framework category:** **Instructional** - reduces interpretation burden on viewer
