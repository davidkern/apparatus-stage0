# Communicative Intents from Tufte Chapter 1 "Graphical Excellence" (pp. 13-32)

Source: Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, Chapter 1 "Graphical Excellence"

---

## Extracted Intents

### 1. Reveal Data

**Definition**: Make visible the underlying patterns, structures, and relationships in data that would otherwise remain hidden in raw numbers or statistical summaries.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Graphics reveal data. Indeed graphics can be more precise and revealing than conventional statistical computations." (p. 13)

**Examples from text**:
- Anscombe's quartet: Four datasets with identical statistical summaries (same means, regression lines, correlation coefficients) that look completely different when graphed (pp. 13-14)
- The scatterplot revealing "point A, a wildshot observation" that "hides in the marginal distribution but shows up as clearly exceptional in the bivariate scatter" (p. 14)

**Framework category**: **Evidential** — The graphic provides evidence that statistics alone cannot convey; it makes the case for data's actual structure.

---

### 2. Enable Comparison

**Definition**: Allow the viewer to easily compare different pieces of data, whether across categories, time periods, or geographic regions.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Graphics should "encourage the eye to compare different pieces of data" (p. 13)

**Examples from text**:
- Cancer mortality maps comparing male vs. female rates (pp. 17-19)
- Anscombe's quartet placed side by side to show four datasets (p. 14)
- Playfair's import/export chart showing "shifting balance of trade by the difference between the import and export time-series" (p. 32)

**Framework category**: **Comparative** — Core comparative intent; the graphic's structure facilitates side-by-side or overlaid comparison.

---

### 3. Communicate Density (Present Many Numbers Compactly)

**Definition**: Convey a large volume of quantitative information in a small visual space, achieving high data density.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Graphics should "present many numbers in a small space" (p. 13); "Only a picture can carry such a volume of data in such a small space" (p. 16)

**Examples from text**:
- Cancer atlas maps: "Each map portrays some 21,000 numbers" (p. 16)
- Galaxy distribution map: "millions of bits of information on a single page" with 1.3 million galaxies mapped (p. 26)
- NYC weather summary: "depicts 1,888 numbers" (p. 30)
- Voyager 2 Jupiter data: "3.6 million bits were reduced... to the 18,900 points actually plotted" (p. 29)

**Framework category**: **Aggregative** — Though not summarizing to a single statistic, this intent aggregates vast quantities into comprehensible form.

---

### 4. Support Multi-Level Reading (Overview to Detail)

**Definition**: Enable viewers to perceive both broad patterns and fine-grained details within the same graphic.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Graphics should "reveal the data at several levels of detail, from a broad overview to the fine structure" (p. 13)

**Examples from text**:
- Cancer maps allowing examination "ranging from the contemplation of general overall patterns to the detection of very fine county-by-county detail" (p. 16)
- Specific observations at multiple scales: national patterns (northeast cancer concentration), regional (Louisiana shipyard workers), and individual counties (Salem County, NJ bladder cancer) (pp. 16-20)

**Framework category**: **Distributional** — Shows shape/spread at multiple granularities; could also be a new category "Scalar" or "Hierarchical Detail"

---

### 5. Make Large Datasets Coherent

**Definition**: Organize and structure complex data so the viewer can comprehend relationships and patterns that would be incomprehensible in raw form.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Graphics should "make large data sets coherent" (p. 13)

**Examples from text**:
- Cancer atlas organizing 3,056 counties' worth of mortality data into interpretable geographic patterns
- Galaxy map making sense of 1.3 million galaxy positions
- The entire chapter's emphasis on graphics that "successfully organizes a large collection of numbers" (p. 30)

**Framework category**: **Aggregative** — Creating coherence from multiplicity is a form of meaningful aggregation.

---

### 6. Direct Attention to Substance

**Definition**: Focus the viewer's cognition on the meaning and implications of the data rather than on graphical technique, methodology, or production technology.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Graphics should "induce the viewer to think about the substance rather than about methodology, graphic design, the technology of graphic production, or something else" (p. 13)

**Examples from text**:
- Cancer maps: "Notice how quickly and naturally our attention has been directed toward exploring the substantive content of the data rather than toward questions of methodology and technique" (p. 20)

**Framework category**: New category suggestion: **Transparency** or subsumes under **Evidential** — The graphic gets out of its own way to let evidence speak.

---

### 7. Preserve Data Integrity (Avoid Distortion)

**Definition**: Represent data faithfully without visual tricks that misrepresent quantities or relationships.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Graphics should "avoid distorting what the data have to say" (p. 13)

**Examples from text**:
- Counter-example: The "Solar Radiation and Stock Prices" chart (p. 15) — "A silly theory means a silly graphic"
- Critique of cancer maps: geographic area creating false visual importance vs. population (p. 20)

**Framework category**: **Evidential** — Truthful representation is essential to evidential integrity.

---

### 8. Generate Hypotheses / Provide Investigative Leads

**Definition**: Suggest causal relationships, anomalies, or patterns worth investigating further.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "The maps provide many leads into the causes—and avoidance—of cancer" (p. 16)

**Examples from text**:
- Louisiana cancer rates suggesting occupational exposure from shipyard asbestos work (p. 16)
- North-central stomach cancer suggesting Scandinavian smoked fish consumption (p. 16)
- Salem County bladder cancer leading to investigation of chemical industry workers (p. 16)
- Galaxy map clusters prompting questions about galactic structure (p. 26)

**Framework category**: **Relational** — Suggests causal/correlational relationships; could also be new category **Exploratory** or **Hypothesis-Generating**

---

### 9. Communicate Spatial Distribution

**Definition**: Show how quantities are distributed across geographic space.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Through extensive treatment of "data maps" as a fundamental graphical form (pp. 16-27)

**Examples from text**:
- Cancer mortality maps across U.S. counties
- Halley's 1686 trade winds and monsoons map (p. 23)
- John Snow's cholera map (p. 24)
- Minard's French wine exports map (pp. 24-25)
- Galaxy distribution map (pp. 26-27)

**Framework category**: **Distributional** — Spatial distribution is a subspecies of distributional intent.

---

### 10. Communicate Temporal Change

**Definition**: Show how quantities change over time, revealing trends, cycles, and periodicities.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "The time-series plot is the most frequently used form of graphic design" with "strength and efficiency of interpretation found in no other graphic arrangement" (p. 28)

**Examples from text**:
- 10th-century planetary orbits graph — "the oldest known example of an attempt to show changing values graphically" (p. 28)
- Lambert's soil temperature periodicity (p. 29)
- Voyager 2 Jupiter radio emissions with 10-hour rotation cycle (p. 29)
- NYC 1980 weather showing daily variation against long-run averages (p. 30)
- Playfair's import/export chart 1700-1782 (p. 32)

**Framework category**: **Temporal** — Core temporal intent.

---

### 11. Show Deviation from Norm/Expected

**Definition**: Display how actual values deviate from baseline, expected, or normal values.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Through examples showing comparison to baselines.

**Examples from text**:
- NYC weather chart: "daily high and low temperatures are shown in relation to the long-run average" (p. 30)
- Cancer maps using statistical significance relative to U.S. average (legend: "Not significantly different from U.S. as a whole" vs. "Significantly higher/lower") (p. 16)

**Framework category**: **Comparative** — Deviation is comparison against a reference.

---

### 12. Identify Outliers/Anomalies

**Definition**: Make exceptional observations visually salient so they can be examined or investigated.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Through the "wildshot" observation example and hot spot detection.

**Examples from text**:
- Point A in scatterplot "hides in the marginal distribution but shows up as clearly exceptional in the bivariate scatter" (p. 14)
- Cancer map "unusual hot spots, including northern Minnesota and a few counties in Iowa and Nebraska" (p. 16)
- "the unusual experience of a county warrants further investigation" (p. 16)

**Framework category**: **Distributional** — Outliers are part of distribution shape; also **Evidential** — anomalies as evidence requiring explanation.

---

### 13. Communicate Direction and Flow

**Definition**: Show movement, direction, or quantity of flow between locations.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Through Halley's wind map and Minard's export flows.

**Examples from text**:
- Halley's trade winds map: "the sharp end of each little stroak pointing out that part of the Horizon, from whence the wind continually comes" (p. 23)
- Minard's wine export map giving "quantity as well as direction to the data measures" (p. 24)

**Framework category**: **Relational** — Flow implies relationship between origin and destination; also **Temporal** when showing movement over time.

---

### 14. Communicate Speed/Rate of Change

**Definition**: Show how quickly quantities change over time or space.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Through Marey's train schedule design.

**Examples from text**:
- Marey's Paris-Lyon train schedule: "The slope of the line reflects the speed of the train: the more nearly vertical the line, the faster the train" (p. 31)
- The TGV overlay showing dramatic speed improvement over 100 years (p. 31)

**Framework category**: **Temporal** — Rate is derivative of temporal change.

---

### 15. Create Lasting Mental Impression

**Definition**: Form a memorable, retainable understanding that persists after viewing.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Through Playfair's argument for graphics over tables.

**Examples from text**:
- Playfair: "On inspecting any one of these Charts attentively, a sufficiently distinct impression will be made, to remain unimpaired for a considerable time, and the idea which does remain will be simple and complete" (p. 32)
- Contrast with tables: "a man who has carefully investigated a printed table, finds... that he has only a very faint and partial idea of what he has read; and that like a figure imprinted on sand, is soon totally erased" (p. 32)

**Framework category**: New category suggestion: **Mnemonic** or **Retention** — Purpose is cognitive persistence.

---

### 16. Integrate Multiple Variables (Multivariate Display)

**Definition**: Show relationships among multiple variables simultaneously.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Excellence, nearly always of a multivariate sort" (p. 15)

**Examples from text**:
- Voyager 2 display: three radio bands, magnetic orientation, distance, and date all on one graphic (p. 29)
- Marey's train schedule: time, space, speed, stops all encoded (p. 31)
- Cancer maps encoding: rate, statistical significance, geography, gender, cancer type (pp. 17-19)

**Framework category**: **Relational** — Multivariate displays show relationships among variables.

---

### 17. Serve Clear Purpose

**Definition**: Have an explicit communicative function rather than existing as decoration or demonstration of technique.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Graphics should "serve a reasonably clear purpose: description, exploration, tabulation, or decoration" (p. 13)

**Examples from text**:
- This is a meta-intent about intentionality itself
- Anti-example: solar radiation/stock prices chart serving no legitimate purpose (p. 15)

**Framework category**: This is a meta-principle about intents rather than an intent itself.

---

### 18. Integrate with Text/Statistics

**Definition**: Work in concert with verbal descriptions and statistical analysis rather than standing in isolation.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Graphics should "be closely integrated with the statistical and verbal descriptions of a data set" (p. 13)

**Examples from text**:
- Cancer atlas maps accompanied by statistical analysis and textual interpretation
- Anscombe's quartet paired with both the data table and statistical summary (pp. 13-14)

**Framework category**: **Instructional** or new category **Contextual** — The graphic is part of a larger communicative system.

## Intent Source Categorization

### From Explicit Prose Statements

These intents were directly articulated in Tufte's bulleted list (p. 13) or explicit prose:

1. **Reveal Data** — "Graphics reveal data"
2. **Enable Comparison** — "encourage the eye to compare"
3. **Communicate Density** — "present many numbers in a small space"
4. **Support Multi-Level Reading** — "reveal data at several levels of detail"
5. **Make Large Datasets Coherent** — "make large data sets coherent"
6. **Direct Attention to Substance** — "induce the viewer to think about the substance"
7. **Preserve Data Integrity** — "avoid distorting what the data have to say"
8. **Serve Clear Purpose** — "serve a reasonably clear purpose"
9. **Integrate with Text/Statistics** — "be closely integrated with statistical and verbal descriptions"

### From Visual Examples (Seen in Figures)

These intents were demonstrated through the visual examples I could examine:

10. **Communicate Spatial Distribution** — Visible in cancer maps, Snow's cholera map, galaxy map
11. **Communicate Temporal Change** — Visible in time-series plots, weather chart, train schedule
12. **Identify Outliers/Anomalies** — Visible in Anscombe's quartet plots, scatterplot with point A
13. **Communicate Direction and Flow** — Visible in Halley's wind map, Minard's wine export map
14. **Communicate Speed/Rate of Change** — Visible in Marey's train schedule (line slopes)
15. **Show Deviation from Norm** — Visible in NYC weather chart (actual vs. normal bands)
16. **Integrate Multiple Variables** — Visible in Voyager 2 multi-panel display, train schedule

### Discoveries from Visual Examination Not Named in Text

These intents emerged from examining the figures but were not explicitly named by Tufte:

- **Encode Statistical Significance Visually** — The cancer maps use a five-level legend combining decile ranking with statistical significance testing. This is visible in the legend but not discussed as a design principle.

- **Use Geography as Organizing Frame** — The maps use familiar geographic shapes (U.S. outline) as a cognitive scaffold. Viewers' existing spatial knowledge aids interpretation. This is implicit in the data map form but not articulated.

- **Layer Information Hierarchically** — The Voyager 2 display stacks multiple related time-series with shared x-axis, creating implicit visual hierarchy. The train schedule uses station spacing proportional to actual distance. These compositional choices are visible but not theorized.

- **Show Simultaneity/Intersection** — Marey's train schedule shows where trains pass each other through line intersections. This specific communicative purpose (when do opposing movements coincide?) is demonstrated visually but only briefly mentioned.

## Notes on Tufte's Design Philosophy

Tufte's chapter establishes several overarching principles that shape how these intents are realized:

1. **Data Primacy**: The data should dominate the visual; design serves revelation, not decoration.

2. **Multivariate Orientation**: Excellence is "nearly always of a multivariate sort" — good graphics show relationships among multiple dimensions.

3. **Comparison as Foundation**: The bulleted list and examples return repeatedly to comparison as the fundamental graphical act.

4. **Skepticism of Summary Statistics**: Anscombe's quartet demonstrates that numerical summaries can deceive; graphics provide necessary evidential supplementation.

5. **Historical Continuity**: Tufte traces graphical forms through history (Lambert, Playfair, Marey) to show that fundamental graphical intents have remained stable even as data volumes have grown.
