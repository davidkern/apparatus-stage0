# Intent Extraction: Tufte Chapter 5-6 (Pages 107-122)

Source: Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*
Pages: 107-122 (Continuation of Chapter 5 "Chartjunk" and beginning of Chapter 6 "Data-Ink Maximization and Graphical Design")

## Extracted Communicative Intents

---

### 1. Visual Clarity Preservation

**Name:** Visual Clarity Preservation

**Definition:** The purpose of preventing perceptual artifacts and optical interference that compromise the viewer's ability to accurately perceive data values.

**How Tufte articulates it:** The chapter section on moire vibration explicitly states these patterns create "an undisciplined ambiguity, with an illusive, eye-straining quality that contaminates the entire graphic. It has no place in data graphical design." (p. 112)

**Specific examples:**
- 3D pyramid chart from New England Journal of Medicine (p. 109) where "some pyramids conceal others" and one variable "has no label or scale"
- JASA style sheet example (p. 110): "The display required 131 line-strokes and 15 digits to communicate its simple information"
- Moiré vibration prevalence table showing 21% of New England Journal of Medicine graphics affected (p. 110)
- Full-page catalog of hatching patterns (p. 111) demonstrating "a few of the many vibrating possibilities"

**Framework category:** **Evidential** - visual interference undermines the reliable transmission of evidence

---

### 2. Signal-to-Noise Optimization

**Name:** Signal-to-Noise Optimization

**Definition:** The purpose of ensuring that visual elements carrying data information are perceptually dominant over administrative or structural elements.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "One of the more sedate graphical elements, the grid should usually be muted or completely suppressed so that its presence is only implicit--lest it compete with the data." (p. 112)

**Specific examples:**
- France population pyramid (p. 113): "This grid camouflages the profile of the data"
- Revised population pyramid where "A revision quiets the grid and gives emphasis to the data"
- Multiwindow plot (p. 114): "The space occupied by the doubled grid lines consumes 18 percent of the area"
- Marey train schedule progression: original (p. 115), thinned grid (p. 115), and gray grid (p. 116)

**Framework category:** **Comparative** - when signal is obscured by noise, comparison and pattern detection fail

---

### 3. Reference Subordination

**Name:** Reference Subordination

**Definition:** The purpose of visually demoting reference elements (grids, axes, frames) below data elements through techniques like graying, thinning, or removal.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "A better treatment, however, is a gray grid" and "When a graphic serves as a look-up table, then a grid may help in reading and interpolating. But even in this case the grids should be muted relative to the data." (p. 116)

**Specific examples:**
- Three versions of Marey train schedule demonstrating progressive grid subordination (pp. 115-116)
- Gray grid version (p. 116) shows train lines clearly while grid recedes
- "A gray grid works well and, with a delicate line, may promote more accurate data reconstruction than a dark grid"
- Multiwindow plot redesign eliminates "optical white dots appear at the intersections" (p. 114)

**Framework category:** **Relational** - proper hierarchy establishes the relationship between data and reference elements

---

### 4. Authentic Representation

**Name:** Authentic Representation (Anti-Duck)

**Definition:** The purpose of ensuring that graphical structure serves data communication rather than decorative, stylistic, or self-promotional goals.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "When a graphic is taken over by decorative forms or computer debris, when the data measures and structures become Design Elements, when the overall design purveys Graphical Style rather than quantitative information, then that graphic may be called a duck." (p. 116)

**Specific examples:**
- Big Duck photograph (p. 117): "For this building the whole structure is itself decoration"
- Venturi quote: "It is all right to decorate construction but never construct decoration"
- 3D College Enrollment chart (p. 118): "five colors report, almost by happenstance, only five pieces of data"
- California Water Atlas graphic (p. 119) identified as a "superbly produced duck"
- We-Used-A-Computer-To-Build-A-Duck syndrome (p. 120)

**Framework category:** **Evidential** - authentic representation is fundamental to honest evidence presentation

---

### 5. Dimensionality Honesty

**Name:** Dimensionality Honesty

**Definition:** The purpose of using only the spatial dimensions genuinely required to represent data, avoiding fake perspective or unnecessary dimensionality that distorts perception.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "The addition of a fake perspective to the data structure clutters many graphics. This variety of chartjunk, now at high fashion in the world of Boutique Data Graphics, abounds in corporate annual reports, the phony statistical studies presented in advertisements, the mass media, and the more muddled sorts of social science research." (p. 118)

**Specific examples:**
- 3D College Enrollment chart (p. 118): "This may well be the worst graphic ever to find its way into print"
- The chart uses five colors and 3D surfaces to show what are essentially five percentage values per year
- California irrigation water graphic (p. 119): elaborate 3D waffle charts on a map

**Framework category:** **Distributional** - false dimensionality distorts perception of distributional shape and magnitude

---

### 6. Technology Subordination

**Name:** Technology Subordination

**Definition:** The purpose of ensuring that graphical technology serves data communication rather than demonstrating technical capability.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "Occasionally designers seem to seek credit merely for possessing a new technology, rather than using it to make better designs... But at least a few computer graphics only evoke the response 'Isn't it remarkable that the computer can be programmed to draw like that?' instead of 'My, what interesting data.'" (p. 120)

**Specific examples:**
- SAS/GRAPH manual: 68% of graphics with moire vibration (p. 112)
- Tell-A-Graf manual: 53% with moire vibration (p. 112)
- Political science journal graphic (p. 120): "thin substance; the clotted, crinkly lettering all in upper-case sans serif; the pointlessly ordered cross-hatching; the labels written in computer abbreviations"
- Table showing historical increase in chartjunk: 1914 texts had 12%, 1980s computer manuals have 53-68% (p. 112)

**Framework category:** **Instructional** - a methodological intent about the proper relationship between tools and goals

---

### 7. Tabular Sufficiency

**Name:** Tabular Sufficiency

**Definition:** The purpose of recognizing when data is best communicated through a table rather than a graphic, choosing the appropriate form for the content.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "Since the information consists of a few numbers and a good many words, it is best to pass up the computerized graphics capability this time and tell the story with a table" (p. 120)

**Specific examples:**
- Political science chart (p. 120) replaced with prose table (p. 121): "Content and tone of front-page articles in 94 U.S. newspapers"
- The table presents the same data more clearly with proper category descriptions
- "The observed values of the percentages should be printed instead" of encoding in bar heights

**Framework category:** **Aggregative** - choosing the right format for efficient summary presentation

---

### 8. Data Engagement

**Name:** Data Engagement (Wonder Provocation)

**Definition:** The purpose of creating graphics that draw the viewer into the data, provoking curiosity and discovery rather than mere passive reception.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "The best designs... are intriguing and curiosity-provoking, drawing the viewer into the wonder of the data, sometimes by narrative power, sometimes by immense detail, and sometimes by elegant presentation of simple but interesting data." (p. 121)

**Specific examples:**
- Cited exemplars: "Minard on Napoleon in Russia, Marey's graphical train schedule, the cancer maps, the Times weather history of New York City, the chronicle of the annual adventures of the Japanese beetle, the new view of the galaxies"
- Contrast with chartjunk: "no information, no sense of discovery, no wonder, no substance is generated by chartjunk"

**Framework category:** **Explanation** - engagement serves the explanatory purpose of graphics, moving beyond mere display to active understanding

---

### 9. Distribution Summary

**Name:** Distribution Summary (Five-Number Display)

**Definition:** The purpose of communicating the central tendency and spread of a distribution through a minimal set of summary statistics visually encoded.

**How Tufte articulates it:** Chapter 6 opens with discussion of Mary Eleanor Spear's "range bar" and John Tukey's "box plot" as methods for displaying "five numbers" (minimum, quartile, median, quartile, maximum). (p. 123)

**Specific examples:**
- Spear's range bar (p. 123): shows range, median, and interquartile range with hatching
- Tukey's box plot (p. 123): box for IQR, whiskers for range, line for median
- Both designs encode the same five-number summary

**Framework category:** **Distributional** - directly addresses how to communicate distributional shape

---

### 10. Ink Efficiency (Quartile Plot)

**Name:** Ink Efficiency

**Definition:** The purpose of conveying the same statistical information with less graphical ink, reducing visual weight without losing data content.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "The revised design, a quartile plot, shows the same five numbers. It is easy to draw by hand or computer and, most importantly, can replace the conventional scatterplot frame." (p. 124)

**Specific examples:**
- Box plot erased to minimal quartile plot (p. 124): single line with offset midspan
- "The straightedge need only be placed on the paper once to draw the quartile plot, compared to six separate placings for the box plot"
- Line weight variation version: "Perhaps special emphasis should be given to the middle half of the distribution"
- Offset middle version: "the preferred form of the quartile plot. It uses the ink effectively and looks good"

**Framework category:** **Aggregative** - efficient summary representation of distributional data

---

### 11. Multi-Criteria Design

**Name:** Multi-Criteria Design (Reasonable Maximization)

**Definition:** The purpose of balancing data-ink maximization with statistical appropriateness and aesthetic quality, not applying any single principle dogmatically.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "In these revisions of the box plot, the principle of maximizing data-ink has suggested a variety of designs, but the choice of the best overall arrangement naturally also rests on statistical and aesthetic criteria--in other words, the procedure is one of reasonable data-ink maximizing." (p. 124)

**Specific examples:**
- Three alternative quartile plot designs presented (p. 124)
- One rejected: "but this design will not work effectively to frame a scatterplot. Nor does it look very good."
- Preferred design chosen for both functional and aesthetic reasons: "uses the ink effectively and looks good"

**Framework category:** **Instructional** - methodological intent about design decision-making

---

### 12. Emphasis Through Structure

**Name:** Emphasis Through Structure

**Definition:** The purpose of using structural variation (line weight, offset, spacing) to create visual emphasis on important data regions without adding decorative elements.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "Perhaps special emphasis should be given to the middle half of the distribution, however, as in the box plot. This can be done by changing line weights... or, even better, by offsetting the middle half" (p. 124)

**Specific examples:**
- Line weight variation in quartile plot (p. 124): heavier line for IQR
- Offset middle design (p. 124): spatial displacement creates emphasis
- Both methods "emphasize" without adding non-data-ink

**Framework category:** **Emphasis** - structural rather than decorative emphasis on important data regions

## Summary: Chartjunk Avoidance as Intent Framework

Tufte's conclusion (p. 121) synthesizes the chapter's lessons into a concise directive:

> Forgo chartjunk, including
> moire vibration,
> the grid, and the duck.

This implies three categories of visual noise to eliminate:
1. **Perceptual artifacts** (moire vibration, optical illusions)
2. **Reference over-presence** (heavy grids that compete with data)
3. **Decorative structure** (ducks that prioritize style over substance)

Chapter 6 then extends the framework to **generative design**: data-ink principles can derive new graphical forms (quartile plots), not just edit existing ones.

## Intent Source Categorization

### From Explicit Prose Statements
These intents were directly articulated by Tufte in text:

1. **Visual Clarity Preservation** - "It has no place in data graphical design" (moire discussion)
2. **Signal-to-Noise Optimization** - "grid should usually be muted or completely suppressed so that its presence is only implicit--lest it compete with the data"
3. **Reference Subordination** - "grids should be muted relative to the data"
4. **Authentic Representation** - full "duck" concept articulated with Venturi quotation
5. **Dimensionality Honesty** - "fake perspective" discussion
6. **Technology Subordination** - "designers seem to seek credit merely for possessing a new technology"
7. **Tabular Sufficiency** - "best to pass up the computerized graphics capability this time and tell the story with a table"
8. **Data Engagement** - "drawing the viewer into the wonder of the data"
9. **Multi-Criteria Design** - "reasonable data-ink maximizing"
10. **Emphasis Through Structure** - "special emphasis should be given to the middle half"

### From Visual Examples I Could See
These intents were demonstrated through the figures:

1. **Visual Clarity Preservation** - The catalog of moire patterns (p. 111) demonstrates the problem visually; the 3D pyramid chart (p. 109) shows occlusion issues where pyramids literally hide each other

2. **Signal-to-Noise Optimization** - The three-version Marey train schedule sequence (pp. 115-116) shows progressive improvement: dark grid -> thinned grid -> gray grid. The data lines become increasingly legible.

3. **Reference Subordination** - France population pyramid before/after (p. 113) shows grid removal revealing the "profile of the data." The multiwindow plot comparison (p. 114) shows how doubled grid lines create optical artifacts.

4. **Authentic Representation** - Big Duck photograph (p. 117) anchors the concept. The California Water Atlas graphic (p. 119) shows a beautiful but informationally compromised map.

5. **Dimensionality Honesty** - College Enrollment 3D chart (p. 118) demonstrates the problem: perspective makes magnitude comparisons impossible; five colors encode what could be a simple two-line chart.

6. **Distribution Summary** - Side-by-side presentation of Spear's range bar and Tukey's box plot (p. 123) enables direct visual comparison of encoding strategies.

7. **Ink Efficiency** - Progressive erasure of box plot to quartile plot (p. 124) shows the same five numbers with decreasing ink; the visual comparison demonstrates no information loss.

8. **Emphasis Through Structure** - The three quartile plot variants (p. 124) show different emphasis strategies: line weight change vs. spatial offset.

### Discoveries from Visual Examination Not Named in Text
These intents emerged from examining the graphics beyond what the prose explicitly names:

1. **Labeling Directness** - The political science graphic (p. 120) uses abbreviated computer labels ("INF", "UNEM", "SHORT") while the replacement table (p. 121) uses full descriptive text. The visual comparison reveals that graphics should use human-readable labels, but this specific intent is shown rather than named.

2. **Scale Legibility** - The political science bar chart (p. 120) has "overly busy vertical scaling shows more percentage markers and labels than there are actual data points." The visual excess of scale lines reveals an intent about matching scale granularity to data granularity.

3. **Data-Form Matching** - The California Water Atlas (p. 119) embeds waffle charts onto a map. Visually, the waffle chart form seems chosen for aesthetic variety rather than data properties. This suggests an intent about matching visualization form to data type, demonstrated but not explicitly theorized.

4. **Historical Progression Awareness** - The table of chartjunk prevalence over time (p. 112) visually demonstrates that computer graphics have worsened rather than improved chartjunk prevalence (12% in 1914 vs 68% in 1980). This implies an intent about learning from graphical history, but the concept is not named as such.

5. **Practical Efficiency** - The quartile plot discussion notes "The straightedge need only be placed on the paper once" versus "six separate placings for the box plot." This reveals an intent about production efficiency not just perceptual efficiency--ease of drawing matters. This is mentioned briefly but not developed as a formal principle.

6. **Aesthetic Quality as Criterion** - "Nor does it look very good" is used to reject a design variant (p. 124). This elevates aesthetic judgment to co-equal status with data-ink ratio, but the relationship between aesthetics and communication remains implicit.
