**Name:** Technology Subordination

**Definition:** The purpose of ensuring that graphical technology serves data communication rather than demonstrating technical capability.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "Occasionally designers seem to seek credit merely for possessing a new technology, rather than using it to make better designs... But at least a few computer graphics only evoke the response 'Isn't it remarkable that the computer can be programmed to draw like that?' instead of 'My, what interesting data.'" (p. 120)

**Specific examples:**
- SAS/GRAPH manual: 68% of graphics with moire vibration (p. 112)
- Tell-A-Graf manual: 53% with moire vibration (p. 112)
- Political science journal graphic (p. 120): "thin substance; the clotted, crinkly lettering all in upper-case sans serif; the pointlessly ordered cross-hatching; the labels written in computer abbreviations"
- Table showing historical increase in chartjunk: 1914 texts had 12%, 1980s computer manuals have 53-68% (p. 112)

**Framework category:** **Instructional** - a methodological intent about the proper relationship between tools and goals
