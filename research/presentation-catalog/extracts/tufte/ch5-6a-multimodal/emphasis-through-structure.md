**Name:** Emphasis Through Structure

**Definition:** The purpose of using structural variation (line weight, offset, spacing) to create visual emphasis on important data regions without adding decorative elements.

**How Tufte articulates it:** "Perhaps special emphasis should be given to the middle half of the distribution, however, as in the box plot. This can be done by changing line weights... or, even better, by offsetting the middle half" (p. 124)

**Specific examples:**
- Line weight variation in quartile plot (p. 124): heavier line for IQR
- Offset middle design (p. 124): spatial displacement creates emphasis
- Both methods "emphasize" without adding non-data-ink

**Framework category:** **Emphasis** - structural rather than decorative emphasis on important data regions

## Summary: Chartjunk Avoidance as Intent Framework

Tufte's conclusion (p. 121) synthesizes the chapter's lessons into a concise directive:

> Forgo chartjunk, including
> moire vibration,
> the grid, and the duck.

This implies three categories of visual noise to eliminate:
1. **Perceptual artifacts** (moire vibration, optical illusions)
2. **Reference over-presence** (heavy grids that compete with data)
3. **Decorative structure** (ducks that prioritize style over substance)

Chapter 6 then extends the framework to **generative design**: data-ink principles can derive new graphical forms (quartile plots), not just edit existing ones.
