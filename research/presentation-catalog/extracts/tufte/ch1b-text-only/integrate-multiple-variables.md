---

**Definition**: Present multivariate data in a unified graphic that allows relationships among several variables to be perceived simultaneously.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Graphical excellence is nearly always multivariate." Describes how graphics can show "multivariate complexity can be subtly integrated into graphical architecture, integrated so gently and unobtrusively that viewers are hardly aware that they are looking into a world of four or five dimensions."

**Examples referenced**:
- Minard's Napoleon campaign graphic (6 variables: army size, 2D location, direction, temperature, dates)
- Los Angeles air pollution small multiples (3 pollutants x 4 times x 2D space)
- Japanese beetle life cycle (space/time on horizontal, depth on vertical)
- Playfair's Statistical Breviary (area, population, revenue, taxation comparison via circles and slope-lines)

**Framework mapping**: Could be a **meta-intent** that applies across categories, or a new category: **Integrative**. The intent is specifically to reveal relationships that only emerge when multiple variables are viewed together.
