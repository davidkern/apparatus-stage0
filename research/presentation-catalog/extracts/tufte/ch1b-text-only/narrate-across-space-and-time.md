---

**Definition**: Tell a story that unfolds across both geographic space and temporal sequence, integrating movement, location, and chronology into a unified narrative.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "An especially effective device for enhancing the explanatory power of time-series displays is to add spatial dimensions to the design of the graphic, so that the data are moving over space (in two or three dimensions) as well as over time."

**Examples referenced**:
- Minard's Napoleon campaign (army's path across geography over time)
- Marey's English rulers timeline (genealogy + history as time-series)
- Japanese beetle life cycle (vertical soil depth + horizontal time/season)

**Framework mapping**: Hybrid of **Temporal** and **Relational** — perhaps a new category **Narrative** or **Spatio-Temporal**. The intent is to convey story, not just data.
