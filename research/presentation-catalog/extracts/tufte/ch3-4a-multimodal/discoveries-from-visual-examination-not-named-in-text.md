These intents emerged from examining the figures but were not explicitly named by Tufte:

- **Use Filled Area to Show Difference** — Playfair's Denmark & Norway chart (p. 92) fills the area between import and export lines with color (yellow for "Balance Against," pink/red for "Balance in Favour of England"), making the trade balance immediately visible as a shape rather than requiring subtraction of two lines. This encoding technique is visible but not theorized.

- **Label Data Directly in the Plot Field** — The organism size/generation time scatterplot places text labels (Whale, Sequoia, E. Coli, etc.) directly adjacent to each data point, eliminating the need for a separate legend and reducing eye movement. This is demonstrated visually but not articulated as a principle.

- **Encode Time Continuously Rather Than Discretely** — The electroencephalogram example shows time as a continuous dimension with ink varying continuously, in contrast to bar charts that discretize time. This distinction between continuous and discrete temporal representation is implicit in the example choice.

- **Use Logarithmic Scales for Multi-Scale Phenomena** — The organism plot uses log scales on both axes to accommodate bacteria (1 micron) to sequoias (100 meters) and generation times from hours to centuries. The scale choice is visible in the axis labels but not discussed as a design decision.
