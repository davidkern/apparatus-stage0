**Definition**: Combine domain knowledge, quantitative skill, and design ability in creating graphics.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Graphical competence demands three quite different skills: the substantive, statistical, and artistic. Yet now most graphical work, particularly at news publications, is under the direction of but a single expertise—the artistic." (p. 87)

**Examples from text**:
- Analogy: "Allowing artist-illustrators to control the design and content of statistical graphics is almost like allowing typographers to control the content, style, and editing of prose." (p. 87)
- Japanese graphical sophistication attributed to "heavy use of statistical techniques in the workplace and extensive quantitative training, even in the early years of school" (p. 84)

**Framework category**: New category suggestion: **Production Quality** — a meta-intent about how graphics should be made.
