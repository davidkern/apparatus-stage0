# Intent Extraction: Tufte Chapter 2 "Graphical Integrity" (pp. 53-65)

Source: Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, Chapter 2 "Graphical Integrity," pages 53-65.

---

## Extracted Communicative Intents

### 1. Truth-Telling (Veridical Representation)

**Definition**: Communicate quantitative information accurately so viewers can learn the truth from the data.

**How Tufte articulates it**:
- "Graphical excellence begins with telling the truth about the data." (p. 53)
- "A graphic does not distort if the visual representation of the data is consistent with the numerical representation." (p. 55)

**Examples from these pages**:
- The Day Mines annual report hiding negative income by using a false baseline (p. 54)
- The airline commission payments graphic comparing 6 months to full years (p. 54)
- The Pittsburgh insane asylum cost comparison treating ordinal as cardinal data (p. 55)

**Framework category**: **Evidential** - This is about establishing and maintaining trust in the evidence presented.

---

### 2. Proportional Representation

**Definition**: Ensure that physical measurements on the graphic surface are directly proportional to the numerical quantities they represent.

**How Tufte articulates it**:
- "The representation of numbers, as physically measured on the surface of the graphic itself, should be directly proportional to the numerical quantities represented." (p. 56)
- Introduction of the **Lie Factor** = size of effect shown in graphic / size of effect in data (p. 57)

**Examples from these pages**:
- Fuel economy standards "road" graphic: 53% data change shown as 783% visual change, Lie Factor = 14.8 (pp. 57-58)
- Oil barrel graphic: 454% increase depicted as 4,280% increase, Lie Factor = 9.4 (p. 62)
- OPEC benchmark prices: 708% increase shown as 6,700%, Lie Factor = 9.5 (p. 62)

**Framework category**: **Evidential** - Proportionality is a constraint on how evidence is encoded to preserve truthfulness.

---

### 3. Contextual Comparison

**Definition**: Place data in meaningful context by showing relevant comparison points, baselines, or reference values.

**How Tufte articulates it**:
- "The non-lying version, in addition, puts the data in a context by comparing the new car standards with the mileage achieved by the mix of cars actually on the road." (p. 59)

**Examples from these pages**:
- Tufte's corrected fuel economy graphic adds "13.7 mpg, average for all cars on road, 1978" and "19.1 mpg, expected average for all cars on road, 1985" (p. 58)
- The Economist's oil price graphic showing nominal vs. real price vs. energy use to GDP ratio (p. 63)

**Framework category**: **Comparative** - The intent is to enable meaningful comparison against relevant benchmarks.

---

### 4. Labeling for Clarity

**Definition**: Use thorough labeling to defeat ambiguity and help viewers correctly interpret the data.

**How Tufte articulates it**:
- "Clear, detailed, and thorough labeling should be used to defeat graphical distortion and ambiguity. Write out explanations of the data on the graphic itself. Label important events in the data." (p. 56)

**Examples from these pages**:
- Playfair's national debt chart labeling historical events: "Revolution," "Accession of Queen Ann," "Beginning of the Spanish War," "End of Continental War," etc. (pp. 64-65)
- Business Week's oil price graphic labeling "Crude oil price adjusted for inflation" (p. 63)

**Framework category**: **Instructional** - Labels guide interpretation and instruct the viewer on how to read the graphic.

---

### 5. Scale Consistency (Show Data Variation, Not Design Variation)

**Definition**: Maintain uniform scales and visual encoding throughout a graphic so that all variation the viewer perceives represents actual data variation.

**How Tufte articulates it**:
- "Show data variation, not design variation." (p. 61)
- "The confounding of design variation with data variation over the surface of a graphic leads to ambiguity and deception, for the eye may mix up changes in the design with changes in the data." (p. 61)

**Examples from these pages**:
- Nobel Prize graphic using irregular time scale (7 decades at 10 years, final period at 4 years) creating false decline (p. 60)
- OPEC oil prices graphic with 5 different vertical scales and 2 different horizontal scales, making same quantity appear 15.1x larger depending on position (p. 61)

**Framework category**: **Temporal** (when involving time) / **Comparative** - Consistent scales enable valid temporal progression and cross-point comparison.

---

### 6. Inflation Adjustment (Monetary Comparability)

**Definition**: When showing monetary values over time, adjust for inflation to enable meaningful comparison of real value.

**How Tufte articulates it**:
- "The only way to think clearly about money over time is to make comparisons using inflation-adjusted units of money." (p. 63)
- Playfair: "This is in real and not in nominal millions" (p. 65)

**Examples from these pages**:
- Multiple oil price graphics showing nominal dollars, missing that real price had declined 1975-1978 (p. 63)
- Business Week, Sunday Times (London), and The Economist correctly showing real vs. nominal prices (p. 63)
- Playfair's 1786 alternative chart showing national debt interest "in real and not in nominal millions" (p. 65)

**Framework category**: **Temporal** - This is specifically about maintaining comparability across time when monetary units are involved.

---

### 7. Pattern Revelation

**Definition**: Reveal underlying patterns, trends, and structure in data that might not be obvious from raw numbers.

**How Tufte articulates it**:
- "Also revealed is a side of the data disguised and mispresented in the original display: the fuel economy standards require gradual improvement at start-up, followed by a doubled rate from 1980 to 1983, and flattening out after that." (p. 59)
- "In the graphic we saw first, the two sources of design variation covered up an intriguing, non-obvious aspect of the data: in the four years prior to the 1979-1980 increases, the real price of oil had declined. Busy with decoration, the graphic had missed the news." (p. 63)

**Examples from these pages**:
- Corrected fuel economy graphic revealing the acceleration and deceleration phases of the standards
- Real-dollar oil price graphics revealing the 1975-1978 price decline masked by nominal-dollar displays

**Framework category**: **Temporal** / **Distributional** - Pattern revelation often concerns temporal trends or distributional shapes.

---

### 8. Reasoning Support (Graphics as Analytical Instruments)

**Definition**: Enable viewers to use graphics as instruments for reasoning about quantitative information, not merely as decoration.

**How Tufte articulates it**:
- Reference to John Tukey: "graphics were used as instruments for reasoning about quantitative information" (p. 53)
- "The special power of graphics comes in the display of large data sets." (p. 56)
- Contrast with "graphics had to be 'alive,' 'communicatively dynamic,' overdecorated and exaggerated" (p. 53)

**Examples from these pages**:
- Tufte's corrected fuel economy chart with connected data points enabling analysis of rate changes
- The Economist's multi-series comparison of nominal price, real price, and energy/GDP ratio

**Framework category**: **Aggregative** / **Relational** - Reasoning support aggregates data for comprehension and reveals relationships.

---

### 9. Honest Decoration (Non-Distorting Embellishment)

**Definition**: When decoration is used, ensure it does not distort the data measures while potentially editorializing about substance.

**How Tufte articulates it**:
- "It is easy enough to decorate these data without lying" (p. 58)
- "Sometimes decoration can help editorialize about the substance of the graphic. But it is wrong to distort the data measures—the ink locating values of numbers—in order to make an editorial comment or fit a decorative scheme." (p. 59)

**Examples from these pages**:
- Tufte's elaborately decorated fuel economy graphic with ornamental frame (p. 59) - "many decorations but no lies"
- Contrast with the "road perspective" fuel economy graphic where decoration causes distortion

**Framework category**: This may require a new category: **Aesthetic** or could be considered a constraint on **Instructional** - decoration should instruct/editorialize without misleading.

---

### 10. Baseline Integrity

**Definition**: Maintain honest baselines that do not hide or obscure data, particularly negative values or the zero point.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Demonstrated through negative example:
- "the case of the disappearing baseline... a negative income in 1970, which is disguised by having the bars begin at the bottom at approximately minus $4,200,000" (p. 54)

**Examples from these pages**:
- Day Mines annual report hiding 1970 loss with false baseline (p. 54)

**Framework category**: **Evidential** - Baseline integrity is a constraint on preserving evidential honesty.

---

### 11. Magnitude Preservation

**Definition**: Represent numerical magnitude, not just order, when data has cardinal (not merely ordinal) properties.

**How Tufte articulates it**:
- "And sometimes the fact that numbers have a magnitude as well as an order is simply forgotten" (p. 55)

**Examples from these pages**:
- Pittsburgh insane asylum costs graphic showing $147, $172, $198, $213, $214 with building images of similar visual size, treating cardinal data as if merely ordinal (p. 55)

**Framework category**: **Comparative** / **Distributional** - Magnitude preservation enables valid comparison and understanding of distribution.

---

### 12. Event Annotation

**Definition**: Mark significant events in the data to help viewers understand causal or contextual relationships.

**How Tufte articulates it**:
- "Label important events in the data." (p. 56)

**Examples from these pages**:
- Playfair's national debt chart with vertical lines and labels for "Revolution," "Accession of Queen Ann," "Accession of George 1st," "Accession of George 2nd," "Beginning of the Spanish War," "End of Continental War," "Beginning of American War," "End of American War" (pp. 64-65)

**Framework category**: **Temporal** / **Relational** - Event annotation establishes temporal context and suggests causal relationships.

## Notes on Framework Extensions

Several intents cluster around what might be called **Integrity Constraints** rather than positive communicative purposes:
- Proportional Representation
- Baseline Integrity
- Scale Consistency
- Magnitude Preservation

These are constraints that must be satisfied for other intents (Comparison, Temporal progression, Distribution) to be achieved honestly. The framework might benefit from distinguishing:
- **Communicative Intents** (what you want to convey)
- **Integrity Constraints** (what must be preserved for that conveyance to be truthful)

Tufte's chapter title "Graphical Integrity" suggests this entire section is about constraints, with the underlying intents being things like "show change over time" or "compare quantities" - but the integrity constraints determine whether those intents are achieved truthfully.

## Multimodal Observations

The PDF contained both text and figures. Several insights emerged specifically from examining the figures:

1. **Lie Factor calculation** required measuring the visual elements (0.6" and 5.3" lines) - this demonstrates that graphical integrity can be empirically verified through physical measurement.

2. **Playfair's charts** (pp. 64-65) are reproduced in color, showing the original hand-coloring with yellow/beige fill under the line and red line for the data series. The visual design choices (wide horizontal format for the "real millions" version vs. tall vertical format for nominal) demonstrate how aspect ratio itself communicates editorial stance.

3. **The corrected charts** Tufte creates are visually simpler than the originals - demonstrating that integrity often correlates with visual restraint.

4. **The decorative frame** around the fuel economy chart (p. 59) with classical ornamental elements shows that decoration and integrity are orthogonal - you can have elaborate visual treatment without distortion.
