# Intent Extraction: Tufte Chapter 1b (pp. 33-52)

Source: "The Visual Display of Quantitative Information," Chapter 1 "Graphical Excellence"
Pages: 33-52 (text extraction, figures not visible)

## Extracted Intents

### 1. REVEAL COMPARISON WITHIN SELF OVER TIME

**Definition**: Show how the same phenomenon changes across different time periods, enabling viewers to compare a variable with its own historical values.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Via Playfair's critique of the bar chart: "it does not compare the same branch of commerce with itself at different periods." Time-series are superior because they "imprint upon the mind that distinct idea" through the "dimension that is formed by duration."

**Examples referenced**:
- Playfair's first bar chart (Scotland imports/exports 1781) — noted as inferior precisely because it lacks temporal self-comparison
- Playfair's 250-year wheat prices chart comparing prices to wages over time

**Framework mapping**: **Temporal** — but specifically self-referential temporal comparison. Related to existing Comparative category but with a temporal axis.

---

### 2. CONVEY CAUSAL EVIDENCE

**Definition**: Present data in a way that moves beyond description toward causal explanation — showing not just that something happened, but implying or demonstrating why.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "The problem with time-series is that the simple passage of time is not a good explanatory variable: descriptive chronology is not causal explanation." Graphics should be "moved toward causal explanation by smuggling additional variables into the graphic design."

**Examples referenced**:
- U.S. House mail volume peaking before elections (time-series with clear causal mechanism)
- Retail sales decomposition showing seasonal, trading-day, and trend components
- Lung cancer vs. cigarette consumption scatterplot

**Framework mapping**: **Evidential** — specifically causal evidence. This extends beyond simple claim+evidence to implying or establishing causation.

---

### 3. INTEGRATE MULTIPLE VARIABLES

**Definition**: Present multivariate data in a unified graphic that allows relationships among several variables to be perceived simultaneously.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Graphical excellence is nearly always multivariate." Describes how graphics can show "multivariate complexity can be subtly integrated into graphical architecture, integrated so gently and unobtrusively that viewers are hardly aware that they are looking into a world of four or five dimensions."

**Examples referenced**:
- Minard's Napoleon campaign graphic (6 variables: army size, 2D location, direction, temperature, dates)
- Los Angeles air pollution small multiples (3 pollutants x 4 times x 2D space)
- Japanese beetle life cycle (space/time on horizontal, depth on vertical)
- Playfair's Statistical Breviary (area, population, revenue, taxation comparison via circles and slope-lines)

**Framework mapping**: Could be a **meta-intent** that applies across categories, or a new category: **Integrative**. The intent is specifically to reveal relationships that only emerge when multiple variables are viewed together.

---

### 4. SHOW BEFORE-AND-AFTER STATE CHANGE

**Definition**: Contrast the state of a system before and after a significant event or intervention, making the change visually evident.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Finally, a vivid design (with appropriate data) is the before-after time-series."

**Examples referenced**:
- Cabrera's candidate magnetic monopole signal (flux before and after detection event)
- Minard's bridge collapse on the Rhone (before/after 1840)

**Framework mapping**: **Comparative** — specifically temporal comparison of states. Overlaps with Temporal but the emphasis is on the contrast created by an event.

---

### 5. NARRATE ACROSS SPACE AND TIME

**Definition**: Tell a story that unfolds across both geographic space and temporal sequence, integrating movement, location, and chronology into a unified narrative.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "An especially effective device for enhancing the explanatory power of time-series displays is to add spatial dimensions to the design of the graphic, so that the data are moving over space (in two or three dimensions) as well as over time."

**Examples referenced**:
- Minard's Napoleon campaign (army's path across geography over time)
- Marey's English rulers timeline (genealogy + history as time-series)
- Japanese beetle life cycle (vertical soil depth + horizontal time/season)

**Framework mapping**: Hybrid of **Temporal** and **Relational** — perhaps a new category **Narrative** or **Spatio-Temporal**. The intent is to convey story, not just data.

---

### 6. ENABLE RAPID COMPARISON VIA CONSISTENT DESIGN (Small Multiples)

**Definition**: Use repeated identical graphical structures to allow viewers to focus on differences in data rather than differences in design, enabling efficient comparison across many cases.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Small multiples are economical: once viewers understand the design of one slice, they have immediate access to the data in all the other slices. Thus, as the eye moves from one slice to the next, the constancy of the design allows the viewer to focus on changes in the data rather than on changes in graphical design."

**Examples referenced**:
- Los Angeles air pollution display (12 time-space-pollutant slices)
- Phillips curve plots for nine countries

**Framework mapping**: **Comparative** — but as a meta-pattern for presentation form. Could also be considered an **Aggregative** intent when the goal is to show a pattern across many instances.

---

### 7. REVEAL RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN ARBITRARY VARIABLES (Relational Graphics)

**Definition**: Plot any variable against any other variable to assess possible relationships, freed from geographic or temporal constraints.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Lambert's breakthrough: "any variable quantity could be placed in relationship to any other variable quantity, measured for the same units of observation." The relational graphic "links at least two variables, encouraging and even imploring the viewer to assess the possible causal relationship between the plotted variables."

**Examples referenced**:
- Lambert's evaporation rate vs. temperature (derived via graphical calculus)
- Lung cancer death rate vs. cigarette consumption
- Thermal conductivity of copper vs. temperature (measurements from multiple labs)
- Phillips curves (unemployment vs. inflation)

**Framework mapping**: **Relational** — this is the core intent of relational graphics. The scatterplot is "the greatest of all graphical designs" for this purpose.

---

### 8. REVEAL DIVERGENCE FROM EXPECTED PATTERN

**Definition**: Show where empirical observations deviate from theoretical expectations or where different measurements disagree.

**How Tufte articulates it**: In discussing Phillips curves: "demonstrating the collapse of what was once thought to be an inverse relationship between the variables." And for copper conductivity: "The very different answers reported in the published literature result mainly from impurities in the samples."

**Examples referenced**:
- Phillips curves showing breakdown of expected inverse relationship
- Thermal conductivity measurements showing inter-laboratory variation

**Framework mapping**: **Evidential** — specifically showing disconfirming or conflicting evidence. Related to Comparative but with an expectation baseline.

---

### 9. COMMUNICATE COMPLEX IDEAS WITH EFFICIENCY

**Definition**: Maximize the ratio of ideas conveyed to resources expended (time, space, cognitive effort).

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Graphical excellence is that which gives to the viewer the greatest number of ideas in the shortest time with the least ink in the smallest space." And: "Graphical excellence consists of complex ideas communicated with clarity, precision, and efficiency."

**Examples referenced**:
- The mail workload graphic is "worth at least 700 words" — the number used in the accompanying news article
- Minard's graphic tells "a rich, coherent story with its multivariate data, far more enlightening than just a single number bouncing along over time"

**Framework mapping**: This is a **meta-principle** rather than a specific intent category. It governs how all intents should be realized.

---

### 10. SYNTHESIZE MULTIPLE SOURCES INTO UNIFIED VIEW

**Definition**: Bring together findings from many separate studies or measurements into a single display that enables comparison and assessment.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Regarding the thermal conductivity graphic: "Note how effectively the graphic organizes a vast amount of data, recording findings of hundreds of studies on a single page and, at the same time, enforcing comparisons of the varying results."

**Examples referenced**:
- Thermal conductivity of copper (measurements from many labs on one plot)

**Framework mapping**: **Aggregative** — specifically meta-analytic aggregation. Combines evidence from multiple sources into a single view.

---

### 11. USE DATA AS ITS OWN MARK (Embodied Data Points)

**Definition**: Make the data point itself carry additional information through its visual form, rather than using abstract marks (dots, lines).

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Two relational designs of a different sort — wherein the data points are themselves data."

**Examples referenced**:
- Lorenz's dog faces showing fear vs. rage interaction
- White pine seedling photographs showing growth vs. calcium levels

**Framework mapping**: Could extend **Relational** or be a distinct sub-intent. The graphic becomes more than encoding — it becomes specimen display.

---

## Anti-Patterns Identified

### Tables Over Graphics for Small Non-Comparative Data

**Tufte's statement**: "small, noncomparative, highly labeled data sets usually belong in tables."

**Implied intent**: Don't use graphics when you lack temporal dimension or comparison — recognize when tabular form serves better.

---

### Belligerent Multivariate Design

**Tufte's statement**: "Occasionally graphics are belligerently multivariate, advertising the technique rather than the data."

**Implied intent**: Multivariate integration should be "gentle and unobtrusive" — technique should serve data, not showcase itself.

## Methodological Limitations

### What Could Not Be Assessed

1. **Visual effectiveness**: Without seeing the graphics, cannot evaluate whether the described intents are actually achieved through the visual form.

2. **Design specifics**: References to line thickness, shading, slope directions, and spatial arrangements cannot be verified.

3. **Anti-pattern identification**: Would likely find more anti-patterns if the "bad" examples were visible.

4. **Subtlety of integration**: Tufte emphasizes that good multivariate graphics are "gentle and unobtrusive" — this quality cannot be assessed without viewing.

5. **Completeness of intent list**: Graphics often communicate intents that their creators don't explicitly articulate. The unseen graphics may embody additional intents not described in the prose.

### OCR Artifacts

The text extraction contains significant OCR noise, particularly in:
- Figure captions and labels (garbled text in image regions)
- Non-English text (Minard's French original)
- Tabular data within figures

These artifacts made some passages difficult to interpret but did not significantly impact extraction of major intents, which came from Tufte's prose commentary.
