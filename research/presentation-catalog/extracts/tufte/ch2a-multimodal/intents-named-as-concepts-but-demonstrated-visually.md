## Notes on Framework Extensions

Several intents cluster around what might be called **Integrity Constraints** rather than positive communicative purposes:
- Proportional Representation
- Baseline Integrity
- Scale Consistency
- Magnitude Preservation

These are constraints that must be satisfied for other intents (Comparison, Temporal progression, Distribution) to be achieved honestly. The framework might benefit from distinguishing:
- **Communicative Intents** (what you want to convey)
- **Integrity Constraints** (what must be preserved for that conveyance to be truthful)

Tufte's chapter title "Graphical Integrity" suggests this entire section is about constraints, with the underlying intents being things like "show change over time" or "compare quantities" - but the integrity constraints determine whether those intents are achieved truthfully.

## Multimodal Observations

The PDF contained both text and figures. Several insights emerged specifically from examining the figures:

1. **Lie Factor calculation** required measuring the visual elements (0.6" and 5.3" lines) - this demonstrates that graphical integrity can be empirically verified through physical measurement.

2. **Playfair's charts** (pp. 64-65) are reproduced in color, showing the original hand-coloring with yellow/beige fill under the line and red line for the data series. The visual design choices (wide horizontal format for the "real millions" version vs. tall vertical format for nominal) demonstrate how aspect ratio itself communicates editorial stance.

3. **The corrected charts** Tufte creates are visually simpler than the originals - demonstrating that integrity often correlates with visual restraint.

4. **The decorative frame** around the fuel economy chart (p. 59) with classical ornamental elements shows that decoration and integrity are orthogonal - you can have elaborate visual treatment without distortion.
