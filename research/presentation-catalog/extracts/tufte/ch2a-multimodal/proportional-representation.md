**Definition**: Ensure that physical measurements on the graphic surface are directly proportional to the numerical quantities they represent.

**How Tufte articulates it**:
- "The representation of numbers, as physically measured on the surface of the graphic itself, should be directly proportional to the numerical quantities represented." (p. 56)
- Introduction of the **Lie Factor** = size of effect shown in graphic / size of effect in data (p. 57)

**Examples from these pages**:
- Fuel economy standards "road" graphic: 53% data change shown as 783% visual change, Lie Factor = 14.8 (pp. 57-58)
- Oil barrel graphic: 454% increase depicted as 4,280% increase, Lie Factor = 9.4 (p. 62)
- OPEC benchmark prices: 708% increase shown as 6,700%, Lie Factor = 9.5 (p. 62)

**Framework category**: **Evidential** - Proportionality is a constraint on how evidence is encoded to preserve truthfulness.
