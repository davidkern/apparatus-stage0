name: Differentiate by Line Weight (Visual Hierarchy through Weight)
---

**Definition**: Use contrasting line weights to encode different types of information, with heavier lines for data and lighter lines for structural elements.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "An effective aesthetic device is the orthogonal intersection of lines of different weights... The contrast in line weight represents contrast in meaning. The greater meaning is given to the greater line weight; thus the data line should receive greater weight than the connecting verticals." (pp. 185-186)

**Examples from text**:
- Mondrian/Diller painting showing orthogonal intersection of different weight lines (p. 185)
- Time-series diagram showing heavier data line vs. lighter connecting verticals (p. 186)
- The logic: "a restatement, in different language, of the principle of data-ink maximization" (p. 186)

**Framework category**: **Emphasis** — Line weight creates visual hierarchy that emphasizes data over structure.
