**Definition**: Design graphics to extend horizontally (wider than tall) to align with natural reading patterns, horizon perception, and causal reasoning conventions.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Graphics should tend toward the horizontal, greater in length than height" (p. 186)

**Examples from text**:
- Four arguments: (1) "analogy to the horizon. Our eye is naturally practiced in detecting deviations from the horizon" (p. 186); (2) "ease of labeling" for left-to-right reading (p. 187); (3) "emphasis on causal influence" with cause on horizontal axis (p. 187); (4) Tukey's counsel that "wiggly curve needs to be wider than tall" (p. 188)
- Playfair's practice: "Of the 89 graphics in six different books by William Playfair, most (92 percent) are wider than tall" (p. 188)

**Framework category**: **Temporal** / **Relational** — Horizontal orientation supports time-series and causal displays; also **Aesthetic Coherence**.
