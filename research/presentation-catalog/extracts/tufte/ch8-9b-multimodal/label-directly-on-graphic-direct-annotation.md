**Definition**: Place identifying labels, explanations, and annotations directly on the graphical surface rather than requiring viewers to decode through separate legends.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "It is nearly always helpful to write little messages on the plotting field to explain the data, to label outliers and interesting data points, to write equations and sometimes tables on the graphic itself, and to integrate the caption and legend into the design" (p. 180)

**Examples from text**:
- The forecasting comparison graphic with institution names placed directly next to their predictions
- Friendly graphics principle: "labels are placed on the graphic itself; no legend is required" vs. unfriendly "obscure codings require going back and forth between legend and graphic" (p. 183)

**Framework category**: **Explanation** — Labels serve the explanatory function of identifying what the viewer is seeing.
