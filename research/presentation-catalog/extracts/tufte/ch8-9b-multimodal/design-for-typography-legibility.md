name: Design for Typography Legibility

**Definition**: Use typographic conventions that maximize readability: upper-and-lower case, serif fonts, modest sizing.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Through the friendly/unfriendly contrast and Josef Albers quotation: "the more the letters are differentiated from each other, the easier is the reading... words consisting of only capital letters present the most difficult reading—because of their equal height, equal volume, and, with most, their equal width." (p. 183)

**Examples from text**:
- Friendly: "type is clear, precise, modest; lettering may be done by hand" and "type is upper-and-lower case, with serifs"
- Unfriendly: "type is clotted, overbearing" and "type is all capitals, sans serif"

**Framework category**: **Emphasis** — Typography affects what gets emphasized and how easily content is absorbed.
