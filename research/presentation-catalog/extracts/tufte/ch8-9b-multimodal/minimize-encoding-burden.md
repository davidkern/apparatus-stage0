---

**Definition**: Reduce the cognitive work required to decode the graphic by avoiding elaborate encoding schemes, cryptic abbreviations, and obscure visual conventions.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Through the friendly/unfriendly contrast: "words are spelled out, mysterious and elaborate encoding avoided" vs. "abbreviations abound, requiring the viewer to sort through text to decode abbreviations" (p. 183)

**Examples from text**:
- Friendly: "elaborately encoded shadings, cross-hatching, and colors are avoided"
- Unfriendly: "obscure codings require going back and forth between legend and graphic"
- Color accessibility: "colors, if used, are chosen so that the color-deficient and color-blind (5 to 10 percent of viewers) can make sense of the graphic" (p. 183)

**Framework category**: **Emphasis** — Reducing encoding burden is a form of ensuring emphasis lands on the data, not the decoding process.
