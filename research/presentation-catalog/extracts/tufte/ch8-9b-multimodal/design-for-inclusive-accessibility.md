**Definition**: Ensure graphics can be understood by viewers with visual differences, including color-deficiency and color-blindness.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "colors, if used, are chosen so that the color-deficient and color-blind (5 to 10 percent of viewers) can make sense of the graphic (blue can be distinguished from other colors by most color-deficient people)" vs. "design insensitive to color-deficient viewers; red and green used for essential contrasts" (p. 183)

**Examples from text**:
- The specific guidance about blue being distinguishable
- The 5-10% statistic establishing this as a significant design consideration

**Framework category**: **Emphasis** / New category: **Accessibility** — Ensuring content reaches all viewers.
