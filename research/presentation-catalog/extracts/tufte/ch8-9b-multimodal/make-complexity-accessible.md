**Definition**: Give viewers access to rich, complex data through design that welcomes rather than repels, opening up the data rather than obscuring it.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Section title "Making Complexity Accessible: Combining Words, Numbers, and Pictures" (p. 180); "An occasional data graphic displays such care in design that it is particularly accessible and open to the eye, as if the designer had the viewer in mind at every turn" (p. 183)

**Examples from text**:
- The "friendly data graphic" concept: graphics that "attract viewer, provoke curiosity" vs. those that are "repellent, filled with chartjunk" (p. 183)
- Contrast between friendly attributes (words spelled out, left-to-right reading, little messages helping explain) and unfriendly (abbreviations requiring decoding, cryptic, vertical text)

**Framework category**: **Emphasis** — Accessibility is about ensuring the important content reaches the viewer; also new category **Hospitality** or **Viewer-Orientation**.
