**Definition**: Communicating that a provision takes precedence over conflicting
provisions.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants the audience to understand that when
conflict exists, this provision wins.

**Legal Formalization**:
- **notwithstanding**: "Notwithstanding any other provision..."
- **shall prevail**: "In case of conflict, this section shall prevail"
- **takes precedence**: "This agreement takes precedence over..."
- **supersedes**: "This agreement supersedes all prior agreements"

**Informal Appearance**:
- "Even though..." (acknowledges conflict)
- "Regardless of..." (overrides other considerations)
- "Despite..." (acknowledges but overrides)
- "This trumps..." (casual override)

**Ambiguity Resolved**: Override establishes hierarchy when rules conflict.
Without explicit override language, conflicts create ambiguity. Legal
language makes precedence explicit.

**Containment**:
- Contains: Prevailing provision reference, Subordinated provisions
- Contained by: Entire agreement clauses, Interpretation provisions

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: No direct analog. This is
meta-communicative—it's about how to interpret other communications
when they conflict.

**Forms**:
- *Typographic*: Often in "General Provisions" section
- *Auditory*: Emphasis on precedence terms
- *Visual*: Hierarchy diagrams for document precedence
