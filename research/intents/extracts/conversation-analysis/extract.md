# Intents from Conversation Analysis and Pragmatics

This document explores communicative intents derived from conversation analysis,
speech act theory, politeness theory, and pragmatics research. These fields
focus on interactive communication, social relationship maintenance, and
implicit meaning.

## New Intent Category: Interactional

Conversation analysis reveals intents focused on managing the interaction itself
rather than conveying information. These are about maintaining the channel,
managing turns, and preserving social relationships.

### Intent: Phatic

**Definition**: Utterance whose primary purpose is establishing or maintaining
social connection rather than conveying propositional content.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to signal social presence, openness to
interaction, and relational warmth. Malinowski's "phatic communion" describes
language used to create "ties of union" through the mere exchange of words.

**Examples**:
- "How's it going?" (not expecting detailed answer)
- "Nice weather we're having."
- "Happy Friday!"
- Status messages in chat: "Good morning team!"
- Slack emoji reactions that signal acknowledgment without content
- "Hey" as conversation opener

**Containment**:
- Contains: (minimal propositional content)
- Contained by: Session, Turn

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Greeting/Farewell (Transition)
but can occur throughout interaction, not just at boundaries. Greeting is about
ritual brackets; Phatic is about social bonding at any point.

---

### Intent: Backchannel

**Definition**: Brief response that signals attention, understanding, or
engagement without claiming a full conversational turn.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to indicate "I'm listening, continue"
without interrupting or shifting to speaker role. Shows active recipiency.

**Examples**:
- "Uh-huh", "mm-hmm", "yeah"
- "Right", "okay", "I see"
- Nodding (in multimodal contexts)
- "Interesting", "wow", "really?"
- Typing indicators in chat
- Short affirmations: "Makes sense", "Got it"

**Containment**:
- Contains: (minimal)
- Contained by: Turn (as listener contribution)

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Acknowledgement (Emphasis)
but lighter. Acknowledgement confirms receipt of specific input; Backchannel
signals ongoing attention. Backchannel is meta-communicative about the channel
itself.

---

### Intent: Continuer

**Definition**: A specific type of backchannel that invites the speaker to
continue their extended turn.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to yield the floor back to the current
speaker, signaling receptivity to more content.

**Examples**:
- "Uh-huh" (with continuing intonation)
- "And then?"
- "Go on..."
- "Tell me more"
- Ellipsis in written chat indicating expectation of more

**Containment**:
- Contains: (minimal)
- Contained by: Turn

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Specialized form of Backchannel.
Distinct because it actively encourages continuation rather than just
acknowledging.

---

### Intent: Pre-Sequence

**Definition**: Preliminary utterance that checks conditions before a main
action, allowing graceful exit if conditions aren't met.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to test feasibility before committing
to a face-threatening request or invitation, preserving face for both parties.

**Examples**:
- "Are you busy right now?" (before request)
- "Do you have a minute?" (before interruption)
- "Have you heard about X?" (before news delivery)
- "Can I ask you something?" (before sensitive question)
- "Is this a good time?" (before substantial interaction)

**Containment**:
- Contains: (checking question)
- Contained by: Request-sequence, Invitation-sequence

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: No direct analog. Could be seen as
Confirmation but oriented toward feasibility rather than approval. Distinct
face-saving function.

---

### Intent: Turn-Yielding

**Definition**: Signal that the current speaker is completing their turn and
making the floor available.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to hand off the conversational floor
to another participant.

**Examples**:
- Falling intonation at end of statement
- "What do you think?"
- "Over to you"
- "Does that make sense?"
- Explicit addressee selection: "John?"
- Completion of adjacency pair first part (question expects answer)

**Containment**:
- Contains: (varies)
- Contained by: Turn

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Marker (Transition) but
specifically about turn management rather than topic structure.

---

### Intent: Turn-Holding

**Definition**: Signal that the current speaker intends to continue despite
natural pause or completion points.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to maintain the floor to complete a
multi-unit turn without interruption.

**Examples**:
- "and um..." (filled pause)
- "wait, there's more"
- "hold on, I'm not done"
- Rising intonation suggesting continuation
- List intonation (non-final items)
- "Three things: first..." (projecting multi-part structure)

**Containment**:
- Contains: (continuation markers)
- Contained by: Turn (extended)

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: No direct analog. Opposite of
Turn-Yielding. Meta-communicative about discourse structure.

## New Intent Category: Face-Work

Brown and Levinson's politeness theory reveals intents focused on managing
"face"--the social self-image people maintain. These intents protect or
enhance face for speaker, hearer, or both.

### Intent: Face-Threatening-Act-Mitigation

**Definition**: Linguistic softening that reduces the impact of an inherently
face-threatening act (request, criticism, disagreement).

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to perform a necessary action (request,
correct, disagree) while minimizing damage to the hearer's positive or
negative face.

**Examples**:
- "I hate to ask, but could you possibly..." (mitigated request)
- "You might want to consider..." (softened suggestion)
- "I could be wrong, but..." (hedged disagreement)
- "Sorry to bother you..." (pre-apology for intrusion)
- Passive voice to avoid naming agent: "Mistakes were made"

**Containment**:
- Contains: Hedge, Apology, Request
- Contained by: Turn

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to hedging in Evidential
(Confidence, Uncertainty) but focused on social face rather than epistemic
state. The uncertainty may be performed rather than genuine.

---

### Intent: Hedge

**Definition**: Linguistic marker that reduces commitment, certainty, or
force of a statement.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to leave room for disagreement, avoid
overprecision, or soften impact through vagueness or qualification.

**Examples**:
- "I think...", "maybe", "sort of", "kind of"
- "It seems like...", "apparently", "arguably"
- "In my opinion...", "from my perspective"
- "Roughly speaking...", "more or less"
- "If I'm not mistaken..."

**Containment**:
- Contains: (qualification markers)
- Contained by: Any assertive statement

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Uncertainty/Confidence
(Evidential) but broader function. Hedges can express genuine uncertainty
OR perform politeness regardless of actual certainty.

---

### Intent: Positive-Face-Enhancement

**Definition**: Utterance that validates, appreciates, or shows interest in
the hearer.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to make the hearer feel valued, liked,
or included--enhancing their positive face needs.

**Examples**:
- "Great question!"
- "That's a really interesting point"
- "I love how you approached this"
- Compliments on work or ideas
- Using inclusive "we" rather than distancing "you"
- Showing interest: "Tell me more about that"

**Containment**:
- Contains: Praise, Acknowledgement
- Contained by: Turn, Feedback

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Praise (Persuasive from
Aristotle) but focused on social maintenance rather than evaluation. Could
also relate to Confidence-Building (Motivation from Instructional Design).

---

### Intent: Negative-Face-Respect

**Definition**: Utterance that acknowledges and protects the hearer's autonomy
and freedom from imposition.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to signal respect for the hearer's
right not to be imposed upon.

**Examples**:
- "Feel free to ignore this, but..."
- "If you have time..."
- "No pressure, but..."
- "I don't want to overstep, but..."
- Giving options rather than directives
- "Just a thought" after a suggestion

**Containment**:
- Contains: Hedge, Option-statement
- Contained by: Request, Suggestion

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Suggestion (Instruction)
but meta-level framing. Not the content but the social positioning around it.

---

### Intent: Self-Deprecation

**Definition**: Downplaying one's own knowledge, abilities, or contributions.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to appear modest, avoid seeming
presumptuous, or create space for correction. Can enhance credibility
paradoxically through displayed humility.

**Examples**:
- "I'm no expert, but..."
- "This might be a dumb question..."
- "I could be totally wrong here"
- "I'm probably missing something, but..."
- "Just my two cents"
- "This is probably obvious, but..."

**Containment**:
- Contains: Hedge, Disclaimer
- Contained by: Claim, Question

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: No direct analog. Related to
Concession (Credibility from Aristotle) but about self rather than argument.
Inverse of Credential.

---

## New Intent Category: Remedial

Goffman's remedial work and speech act theory reveal intents focused on
repairing social breaches. These restore equilibrium after face-threatening
events.

### Intent: Apology

**Definition**: Expression accepting responsibility for an offense and
expressing regret.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to acknowledge wrongdoing and repair
the social relationship damaged by the offense.

**Examples**:
- "I'm sorry for the confusion"
- "My apologies for the delay"
- "I apologize for any inconvenience"
- "Sorry about that" (casual)
- "I take full responsibility"

**Containment**:
- Contains: Acknowledgement (of offense), Expression of regret
- Contained by: Remedial interchange

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Extends Acknowledgement (Emphasis)
but includes regret and responsibility. Related to Defense (Persuasive from
Aristotle) but admits rather than justifies.

---

### Intent: Excuse

**Definition**: Explanation that admits the wrongfulness of an act but denies
full responsibility.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to mitigate blame by explaining
external factors that contributed to the offense.

**Examples**:
- "The server was down, so I couldn't submit in time"
- "I didn't receive the notification"
- "I wasn't aware that was a requirement"
- "The documentation wasn't clear"
- "There were circumstances beyond my control"

**Containment**:
- Contains: Causation (external attribution), Apology (often partial)
- Contained by: Remedial interchange

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Defense (Persuasive from
Aristotle) but specifically about responsibility mitigation. Related to
Explanation but with exculpatory purpose.

---

### Intent: Justification

**Definition**: Explanation that admits responsibility but denies the
wrongfulness of the act.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to reframe the apparent offense as
actually appropriate given the circumstances.

**Examples**:
- "I had to break the API because the security fix required it"
- "The delay was necessary to ensure quality"
- "I prioritized the critical bug over the feature request"
- "Sometimes you have to break things to make progress"
- "It was the lesser of two evils"

**Containment**:
- Contains: Claim+Evidence, Causation
- Contained by: Remedial interchange

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Very close to Defense (Persuasive
from Aristotle). May be identical or Defense could be seen as broader
container.

---

### Intent: Disclaimer

**Definition**: Prospective statement that anticipates and preempts potential
negative evaluation.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to protect face before an action by
signaling awareness of potential problems.

**Examples**:
- "I haven't tested this, but..."
- "This is just a rough draft"
- "Off the top of my head..."
- "I'm still learning this, but..."
- "This might be controversial..."
- "Devil's advocate here..."

**Containment**:
- Contains: Self-Deprecation (often), Hedge
- Contained by: Pre-sequence to claim or action

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Anticipation (Argumentative
from Aristotle) but about self-protection rather than objection-handling.
Prospective face-work.

---

## New Intent Category: Preference-Marked

Conversation analysis reveals that responses to invitations, requests, and
offers are structurally marked as "preferred" (acceptance) or "dispreferred"
(refusal). This marking is about social solidarity.

### Intent: Dispreferred-Response-Preface

**Definition**: Markers that signal an upcoming response that threatens
social solidarity (refusal, disagreement, bad news).

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to prepare the hearer for potentially
disappointing content, softening its impact.

**Examples**:
- "Well..." (with delay) before declining
- "Uh, I don't know..." before disagreement
- "The thing is..." before bad news
- Pauses and hesitation before refusals
- "I appreciate you asking, but..."
- "I wish I could, but..."

**Containment**:
- Contains: Hedge, Delay markers
- Contained by: Response turn

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: No direct analog. Meta-communicative
about what's coming. Related to Face-Threatening-Act-Mitigation but specifically
as response structure.

---

### Intent: Account

**Definition**: Explanation provided with or for a dispreferred response.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to justify a face-threatening response
(refusal, disagreement) by providing reasons.

**Examples**:
- "I can't make it because I have another commitment"
- "I disagree--here's why..."
- Explanation following a "no"
- "The reason I can't is..."

**Containment**:
- Contains: Excuse, Justification, Explanation
- Contained by: Dispreferred response

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Container for Excuse/Justification.
The obligation to account is a structural feature of dispreferred responses.

## New Intent Category: Implicature

Grice's work reveals intents that communicate beyond literal meaning through
deliberate violation or exploitation of conversational expectations.

### Intent: Indirect-Speech-Act

**Definition**: Utterance that performs one speech act on the surface while
intending a different one.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to accomplish an action (typically a
request) while appearing to do something less imposing (ask a question).

**Examples**:
- "Can you pass the salt?" (request as ability question)
- "Would you mind closing the door?" (request as willingness question)
- "It's cold in here" (request to close window as statement)
- "Do you have a watch?" (request for time as yes/no question)
- "I was wondering if you might be able to..." (embedded request)

**Containment**:
- Contains: Surface act (question), Intended act (request/directive)
- Contained by: Request-sequence

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Suggestion (Instruction)
but specifically about the indirection mechanism. The indirectness itself
has communicative significance.

---

### Intent: Hint

**Definition**: Utterance that indirectly conveys meaning by relying on
contextual inference rather than explicit statement.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to communicate something without
saying it directly, preserving deniability or avoiding imposition.

**Examples**:
- Mentioning a problem without explicitly requesting help
- "Nice code you have there" (implying criticism through marked praise)
- "The tests are taking a while to run" (implying concern about complexity)
- Leaving relevant information conspicuously absent
- "Some people might think that's an issue..."

**Containment**:
- Contains: (the hint carrier)
- Contained by: Turn

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Distinct from explicit forms. Related
to instructional Hint (Guidance from Instructional Design) but that is about
graduated support; this is about social indirection.

---

### Intent: Flouting

**Definition**: Deliberately obvious violation of a conversational maxim to
convey implicature.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to communicate something through the
recognizable violation of expected cooperative behavior.

**Examples**:
- Obvious sarcasm (violating quality maxim)
- Answering with excessive irrelevance (violating relation maxim)
- Giving obviously incomplete information (violating quantity maxim)
- Being deliberately obscure (violating manner maxim)
- Overstatement and understatement

**Containment**:
- Contains: (the flouting mechanism)
- Contained by: Turn

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: No direct analog. Meta-communicative
through deliberate norm violation.

---

## New Intent Category: Topic-Management

Conversation analysis reveals intents for managing what a conversation is about.

### Intent: Topic-Initiation

**Definition**: Introduction of a new conversational topic.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to establish a new focus for the
conversation, typically by making a topic available and checking recipient
uptake.

**Examples**:
- "Did you hear about...?"
- "Speaking of which..."
- "Have you seen the latest on X?"
- News announcements: "Guess what happened"
- Elicitors: "What's new?"

**Containment**:
- Contains: (topic carrier)
- Contained by: Turn

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Marker (Transition) but
specifically about topic introduction rather than generic boundary.

---

### Intent: Topic-Shift

**Definition**: Signal that moves conversation from one topic to another.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to change the conversational focus,
either stepwise (gradual) or boundaried (explicit).

**Examples**:
- "Anyway..." (boundaried shift)
- "That reminds me..." (stepwise connection)
- "On a different note..."
- "Before I forget..."
- "Oh, I meant to tell you..."

**Containment**:
- Contains: Marker, Topic-Initiation
- Contained by: Turn

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Close to Marker (Transition) but
specifically about topic structure rather than generic structure.

---

### Intent: Topic-Closure

**Definition**: Signal that a topic is being wound down or terminated.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to end discussion of the current topic,
making space for shift or termination.

**Examples**:
- "So, anyway..." (summary + transition)
- "Right, so that's that"
- "Well, I think we've covered that"
- Assessment tokens: "Good", "Fine", "Right"
- Summarizing before moving on

**Containment**:
- Contains: Summary (often), Marker
- Contained by: Turn, Section

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Summary (Transition)
when summary is used for closure. Also related to Farewell (for
conversation-level closure).

## Intents from Searle's Taxonomy

Searle's five categories of illocutionary acts suggest basic intent types.

### Intent: Commissive

**Definition**: Utterance that commits the speaker to a future course of action.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to bind themselves to do something,
creating an obligation.

**Examples**:
- Promises: "I'll have it done by Friday"
- Offers: "Let me handle that"
- Vows: "I commit to maintaining this"
- Threats: "If you do X, I will do Y"
- Contracts and agreements

**Containment**:
- Contains: Future action description
- Contained by: Sequence

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Command/Step (Instruction)
but self-directed. The direction of obligation is toward speaker rather than
hearer. Partially covered by Error Handling (Suggestion as future commitment
to fix).

---

### Intent: Expressive

**Definition**: Utterance that expresses a psychological state about a state
of affairs.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to communicate how they feel about
something--gratitude, sympathy, congratulation, condolence.

**Examples**:
- "Thank you" (gratitude)
- "Congratulations on the launch" (joy at other's success)
- "Sorry to hear that" (sympathy)
- "Welcome to the team" (greeting with positive affect)
- "That's frustrating" (sharing emotion)
- "Good luck" (well-wishing)

**Containment**:
- Contains: (psychological state expression)
- Contained by: Turn, Feedback

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Emotional (from Aristotle
discovery) but Expressive is about communicating speaker's state, while
Emotional intents (Reassurance, Urgency, Empathy) aim to affect hearer's state.

---

### Intent: Declaration

**Definition**: Utterance that changes institutional or social reality by
being uttered.

**Semantic Purpose**: The author wants to bring about a new state of affairs
through the performative power of the utterance itself.

**Examples**:
- "You're hired" / "You're fired"
- "I pronounce this version 2.0"
- "This feature is now deprecated"
- "Meeting adjourned"
- "I resign"
- "The issue is closed"

**Containment**:
- Contains: (the performative)
- Contained by: (institutional context)

**Relationship to Existing Categories**: Related to Status (State) but
performative rather than descriptive. Status reports current state;
Declaration changes it.

### Unique Contributions

Conversation analysis contributes intents not found in other research domains:

1. **Interactional intents** (Backchannel, Continuer, Turn-management): These
   are specifically about managing the communication channel itself, not
   content.

2. **Face-work intents**: While Aristotle covers persuasion and instructional
   design covers learning, neither systematically addresses the social
   maintenance work of protecting self-image.

3. **Preference structure**: The marking of responses as preferred/dispreferred
   is a structural phenomenon specific to interactive dialogue.

4. **Phatic function**: While related to Greeting/Farewell, phatic communication
   as ongoing social maintenance is distinct.

5. **Implicature mechanisms**: The distinction between direct and indirect
   speech acts, and between genuine and performed uncertainty, is unique to
   pragmatics.

## Open Questions

1. **Are interactional intents a category or a dimension?** Channel maintenance
   happens simultaneously with content delivery. Should "interactional" be
   orthogonal to content categories?

2. **Face-work as universal constraint?** Brown and Levinson suggest all
   communication navigates face concerns. Should face-saving be a modifier
   on all intents rather than a separate category?

3. **Multimodality**: Many interactional signals are non-verbal (nods, gaze,
   typing indicators). How does the intent framework handle intents that may
   have no linguistic realization?

4. **Sincerity vs. performance**: Hedges and self-deprecation can be genuine
   OR performed (e.g., false modesty). Does the framework need to distinguish
   sincere vs. strategic uses?

5. **Adjacency pairs as container**: Conversation analysis treats
   question-answer, request-compliance, etc. as structural pairs. Should
   "Adjacency-Pair" be a container intent encompassing First-Pair-Part and
   Second-Pair-Part?

6. **Cultural variation**: Politeness strategies vary significantly across
   cultures. Should the framework include culture-relative variants of
   face-work intents?

7. **Real-time vs. asynchronous**: Turn-management intents assume synchronous
   interaction. How do they adapt to asynchronous formats (email, GitHub
   comments)?

8. **Relationship to "tone"**: Many of these intents are about HOW something
   is said rather than WHAT is said. Is there a tone/register dimension
   orthogonal to intent categories?

## Implications for the Framework

Conversation analysis suggests several expansions to the existing framework:

### 1. Add Interactional Category

The current framework focuses on information transfer. Conversation analysis
reveals that significant communicative work goes into managing the interaction
itself: who speaks, when, how long, and with what social tenor.

Proposed intents:
- Phatic (social bonding)
- Backchannel (active listening)
- Continuer (floor yield to speaker)
- Turn-Yielding (floor offer)
- Turn-Holding (floor retention)
- Pre-Sequence (feasibility check)

### 2. Add Face-Work Category or Dimension

Politeness theory suggests that all communication navigates social face
concerns. This could be:
- A new category containing face-related intents, OR
- A dimension that modifies other intents (a Warning can be delivered with
  more or less face-work)

Proposed intents:
- Face-Threatening-Act-Mitigation
- Positive-Face-Enhancement
- Negative-Face-Respect
- Self-Deprecation

### 3. Expand Transition Category

Topic management intents fit naturally with existing Transition intents but
add specificity:
- Topic-Initiation (not just boundary but topic introduction)
- Topic-Shift (change focus)
- Topic-Closure (wind down topic)

### 4. Expand Emphasis Category with Remedial Intents

Remedial work intents address problems in social equilibrium:
- Apology (extends Acknowledgement)
- Excuse (explains without full responsibility)
- Justification (defends act as appropriate)
- Disclaimer (prospective face protection)

### 5. Consider Implicit/Explicit Dimension

Gricean implicature suggests that the same intent can be realized directly
or indirectly:
- Indirect-Speech-Act
- Hint
- Flouting

This may be a dimension (degree of directness) rather than separate intents.

### 6. Integrate Searle's Categories

Searle's taxonomy maps partially to existing structure:
- Assertives -> Evidential/State
- Directives -> Instruction
- Commissives -> (new: self-directed commitment)
- Expressives -> Emotional
- Declarations -> State (performative)

The gap is **Commissives**--speaker self-commitment--which is common in
interactive contexts ("I'll look into that").
