# Communicative Intents from Tufte Chapters 3-4 (pp. 79-92)

Source: Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, Chapter 3 "Sources of Graphical Integrity and Sophistication" (pp. 79-87) and Chapter 4 "Data-Ink and Graphical Redesign" (pp. 91-94)

---

## Extracted Intents

### 1. Explain Relationships (Relational Graphics)

**Definition**: Show how one variable affects another, confronting claims about cause and effect with visual evidence.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Relational graphics are essential to competent statistical analysis since they confront statements about cause and effect with evidence, showing how one variable affects another." (p. 82)

**Examples from text**:
- Scatterplot of walking speed vs. city population size (p. 82): Shows cities from six countries plotting pace of life against population, revealing that "the bigger the city, the faster its inhabitants walk"
- Japanese economics textbook charts showing industry price indices vs. production ratios (p. 82)
- Scientific graphics from *Science* journal achieving 42% relational design rate (p. 83)

**Framework category**: **Relational** — This is the core relational intent; graphics exist specifically to show variable-to-variable relationships.

---

### 2. Confront Cause-and-Effect Claims with Evidence

**Definition**: Provide visual grounds for evaluating causal assertions, making it possible to test whether claimed relationships hold in the data.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Relational graphics "confront statements about cause and effect with evidence" (p. 82)

**Examples from text**:
- The "Views on the Economy Influence Carter Support" graphic (p. 81): Attempts to show economic perception's effect on voting, though Tufte criticizes its execution
- Walking speed vs. population study: The graphic provides evidence for the hypothesis that city size affects pace of life

**Framework category**: **Evidential** — The graphic serves as evidence against which claims can be tested.

---

### 3. Explain Data (Explanatory Graphics)

**Definition**: Help audiences understand why patterns exist, not merely what the patterns are.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "The relational graphic, unlike the simpler designs, is an explanatory graphic—surely a natural for news reporting and analysis." (p. 84)

**Examples from text**:
- Contrast between time-series (showing what happened) and relational graphics (showing why)
- The critique that news graphics rarely achieve explanatory purpose, instead merely showing sequence

**Framework category**: **Relational** — Explanation requires showing relationships, not just presenting values.

---

### 4. Respect Audience Intelligence

**Definition**: Present data with sufficient complexity and density to match the audience's capacity for understanding, rather than dumbing down.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Quotes E.B. White: "No one can write decently who is distrustful of the reader's intelligence, or whose attitude is patronizing." Applies this to graphics: "Contempt for graphics and their audience, along with the lack of quantitative skills among illustrators, has deadly consequences for graphical work." (p. 81)

**Examples from text**:
- Critique of "The Company Cafeteria was used by 9 Out of 10 Employees" pictograph (p. 81) — an "unnecessary chart" that insults the audience
- Evidence that 80% of Sunday New York Times readers attended college, yet receive graphics at "pre-adult level of intelligence" (p. 84)
- Japanese children's contest entries involving relational designs (7-year-olds graphing mother-child play frequency correlated with reasons given) (p. 84)

**Framework category**: New category suggestion: **Epistemic Respect** or subsumed under **Evidential** — treating audience as capable of handling evidence.

---

### 5. Find and Present the Right Numbers

**Definition**: Ensure the data being visualized are the substantively meaningful numbers that reveal the actual story, not just convenient or decorative data.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "If the statistics are boring, then you've got the wrong numbers. Finding the right numbers requires as much specialized skill—statistical skill—and hard work as creating a beautiful design or covering a complex news story." (p. 80)

**Examples from text**:
- Counter-examples: News graphics that miss "the real news actually in the data" (p. 87)
- Anti-pattern: graphics that "lure the reader's attention away from the advertising" rather than "explain the news in any detail" (p. 80)

**Framework category**: New category suggestion: **Substantive Selection** or **Evidential** — the graphic communicates only insofar as it presents the right evidence.

---

### 6. Achieve Graphical Sophistication

**Definition**: Use graphic forms appropriate to the complexity of the data and the analytical questions at hand.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Measures "graphical sophistication" as "the share of a publication's graphics that are relational" (p. 82). Relational graphics are "essential to competent statistical analysis" while "not quite as simple as the bar chart, time-series plot, or data map" (p. 82).

**Examples from text**:
- Table 1 ranking 15 news publications by percentage of relational graphics (p. 83)
- Scientific journals (*Science*) at 42% relational vs. U.S. newspapers at 0.0-0.6%
- Japanese newspapers (Akahata at 9.3%, Asahi at 7.6%) outperforming U.S. and European counterparts

**Framework category**: **Relational** — Sophistication is measured by capacity for relational display.

---

### 7. Avoid Decoration Without Data

**Definition**: Ensure graphic elements serve data communication rather than mere ornamentation or attention-getting.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Critiques the "doctrine of boring data" that leads to "an Art Department and an Art Director... required to animate the data" (p. 80). "As the art bureaucracy grows, style replaces content." (p. 80)

**Examples from text**:
- The cafeteria pictograph using repeated human figures to show 90% — pure decoration (p. 81)
- Art director quote: graphics "intended more to lure the reader's attention away from the advertising than to explain the news" (p. 80)
- Critique of "Plain statistics embellished or humanized with pictures" (p. 80)

**Framework category**: **Evidential** — Decoration without data undermines evidential function. Also relates to Data-Ink concept from Chapter 4.

---

### 8. Show the Data (Above All Else)

**Definition**: The fundamental principle that data graphics must prioritize displaying the quantitative information itself over all other design considerations.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Above all else show the data." (p. 92) — stated as "the fundamental principle of good statistical graphics" and "the basis for a theory of data graphics."

**Examples from text**:
- Playfair's evolution from cluttered early charts (August 1785) with "elaborate grid lines and detailed labels" to clean later designs (May 1786) that "focused attention on the time-series itself" (pp. 91-92)
- Denmark & Norway import/export chart (1786) as exemplar of data-focused design (p. 92)

**Framework category**: **Evidential** — This is the meta-principle underlying all evidential communication.

---

### 9. Maximize Data-Ink Ratio

**Definition**: Devote the maximum possible proportion of a graphic's ink to representing data rather than non-data elements.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "A large share of ink on a graphic should present data-information, the ink changing as the data change. Data-ink is the non-erasable core of a graphic, the non-redundant ink arranged in response to variation in the numbers represented." (p. 93)

**Formula given**:
- Data-ink ratio = data-ink / total ink used to print the graphic
- = proportion of a graphic's ink devoted to non-redundant display of data-information
- = 1.0 - proportion of a graphic that can be erased without loss of data-information

**Examples from text**:
- Electroencephalogram with eight continuous tracks: "Nothing can be erased without losing information" (p. 93)
- Organism size vs. generation time scatterplot: "Most of the ink in this graphic is data-ink (the dots and labels on the diagonal), with perhaps 10-20 percent non-data-ink (the grid ticks and the frame)" (p. 94)
- Counter-example: Voter registration prediction chart where "the grid sea overwhelms the numbers (the faint points scattered about the diagonal)" (p. 94)

**Framework category**: **Evidential** — Data-ink ratio is a measure of commitment to evidence over apparatus.

---

### 10. Help People Reason About Quantitative Information

**Definition**: Serve as cognitive instruments that facilitate analytical thinking about numbers.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Essentially statistical graphics are instruments to help people reason about quantitative information." (p. 91)

**Examples from text**:
- Contrast with "graphics worthy of the Museum of Modern Art" — art appreciation is not the goal
- The entire Chapter 4 introduction frames graphics as reasoning tools

**Framework category**: **Instructional** or **Evidential** — Graphics enable the cognitive work of understanding evidence.

---

### 11. Draw Attention to Sense and Substance

**Definition**: Direct the viewer's cognitive attention toward meaning and implication rather than toward the graphic itself.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Data graphics should draw the viewer's attention to the sense and substance of the data, not to something else. The data graphical form should present the quantitative contents." (p. 91)

**Examples from text**:
- Playfair's design evolution: moving from apparatus-heavy to data-focused
- The principle that design should be invisible, serving data

**Framework category**: **Evidential** — Transparency of form allows evidence to communicate directly.

---

### 12. Integrate Substantive, Statistical, and Artistic Expertise

**Definition**: Combine domain knowledge, quantitative skill, and design ability in creating graphics.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Graphical competence demands three quite different skills: the substantive, statistical, and artistic. Yet now most graphical work, particularly at news publications, is under the direction of but a single expertise—the artistic." (p. 87)

**Examples from text**:
- Analogy: "Allowing artist-illustrators to control the design and content of statistical graphics is almost like allowing typographers to control the content, style, and editing of prose." (p. 87)
- Japanese graphical sophistication attributed to "heavy use of statistical techniques in the workplace and extensive quantitative training, even in the early years of school" (p. 84)

**Framework category**: New category suggestion: **Production Quality** — a meta-intent about how graphics should be made.

---

### 13. Communicate Tremendous Power

**Definition**: Leverage the unique capacity of graphics to convey quantitative observation more effectively than words.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "It wastes the tremendous communicative power of graphics to use them merely to decorate a few numbers. Moreover, much of the world these days is observed and assessed quantitatively—and well-designed graphics are far more effective than words in showing such observations." (p. 87)

**Examples from text**:
- This is articulated as the waste involved in poor graphics rather than demonstrated by example

**Framework category**: **Evidential** — The communicative power exists specifically for conveying evidence.

---

### 14. Present Quantitative Contents Faithfully

**Definition**: Ensure the graphical form accurately represents the quantities being shown.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "The data graphical form should present the quantitative contents." (p. 91)

**Examples from text**:
- Playfair's charts where the line positions correspond to the actual import/export values
- Implicit in the distinction between data-ink (responds to data variation) and non-data-ink

**Framework category**: **Evidential** — Faithful representation is the foundation of evidential communication.

## Anti-Patterns Revealing Purposes by Negation

Tufte identifies several anti-patterns that reveal communicative intents by showing their violation:

### Anti-Pattern: Graphics as Diversion

**What it reveals**: Graphics should explain, not distract.

**Tufte's description**: Art director who says graphics "are intended more to lure the reader's attention away from the advertising than to explain the news" (p. 80)

**Violated intent**: Explain Data

---

### Anti-Pattern: Instant Understandability Requirement

**What it reveals**: Graphics can and should support complex understanding.

**Tufte's description**: News director who says "graphics must be instantly understandable: 'If you have to explain it, don't use it'" (p. 81)

**Violated intent**: Respect Audience Intelligence, Achieve Graphical Sophistication

---

### Anti-Pattern: Bivariate Data Forced into Univariate Design

**What it reveals**: Form should match data structure.

**Tufte's description**: "In seeking to avoid the subtleties of the scatterplot, artists drew up these convoluted specimens, forcing bivariate data into a univariate design" (p. 81)

**Violated intent**: Explain Relationships, Achieve Graphical Sophistication

**Visual example**: The "Comparative Tax Loads and Economic Growth Rates" chart (p. 81) and the "Views on the Economy Influence Carter Support" paired bar charts — both represent two-variable relationships but refuse to use two-dimensional plotting.

---

### Anti-Pattern: Grid Overwhelming Data

**What it reveals**: Data should dominate visual field.

**Tufte's description**: Voter registration chart where "the grid sea overwhelms the numbers (the faint points scattered about the diagonal)" (p. 94)

**Violated intent**: Maximize Data-Ink Ratio, Show the Data

---

### Anti-Pattern: Elaborate Non-Data Detail

**What it reveals**: Non-data elements should be minimized.

**Tufte's description**: Playfair's early charts "devoted too much of their ink to graphical apparatus, with elaborate grid lines and detailed labels" (p. 91)

**Violated intent**: Maximize Data-Ink Ratio

---

## Intent Source Categorization

### From Explicit Prose Statements

These intents were directly articulated in Tufte's prose:

1. **Explain Relationships** — "confront statements about cause and effect with evidence" (p. 82)
2. **Explain Data** — "an explanatory graphic" (p. 84)
3. **Respect Audience Intelligence** — E.B. White quote and application (p. 81)
4. **Find and Present the Right Numbers** — "If the statistics are boring, then you've got the wrong numbers" (p. 80)
5. **Show the Data** — "Above all else show the data" (p. 92)
6. **Maximize Data-Ink Ratio** — Formal definition and formula (p. 93)
7. **Help People Reason** — "instruments to help people reason about quantitative information" (p. 91)
8. **Draw Attention to Sense and Substance** — "draw the viewer's attention to the sense and substance" (p. 91)
9. **Integrate Three Expertises** — "substantive, statistical, and artistic" (p. 87)
10. **Communicate Tremendous Power** — "tremendous communicative power of graphics" (p. 87)

### From Visual Examples (Seen in Figures)

These intents were demonstrated through visual examples I examined:

11. **Show Bivariate Relationships** — Visible in walking speed vs. population scatterplot (p. 82) showing labeled points across six countries on log-log axes

12. **Continuous Data Recording** — Visible in electroencephalogram (p. 93) where eight channels show unbroken traces of brain activity, every pixel of ink representing data

13. **Direct Labeling** — Visible in organism size/generation time plot (p. 94) where organisms are labeled directly at their data points (Whale, Fir, E. Coli, etc.) rather than using a legend

14. **Clean Time-Series Design** — Visible in Playfair's improved Denmark & Norway chart (p. 92) with minimal grid, clear filled areas showing trade balance, and uncluttered axis treatment

15. **Data Primacy Through Visual Weight** — Visible in the contrast between the good (organism plot) and bad (voter registration) scatterplots on p. 94 — in one, data points dominate; in the other, grid dominates

### Discoveries from Visual Examination Not Named in Text

These intents emerged from examining the figures but were not explicitly named by Tufte:

- **Use Filled Area to Show Difference** — Playfair's Denmark & Norway chart (p. 92) fills the area between import and export lines with color (yellow for "Balance Against," pink/red for "Balance in Favour of England"), making the trade balance immediately visible as a shape rather than requiring subtraction of two lines. This encoding technique is visible but not theorized.

- **Label Data Directly in the Plot Field** — The organism size/generation time scatterplot places text labels (Whale, Sequoia, E. Coli, etc.) directly adjacent to each data point, eliminating the need for a separate legend and reducing eye movement. This is demonstrated visually but not articulated as a principle.

- **Encode Time Continuously Rather Than Discretely** — The electroencephalogram example shows time as a continuous dimension with ink varying continuously, in contrast to bar charts that discretize time. This distinction between continuous and discrete temporal representation is implicit in the example choice.

- **Use Logarithmic Scales for Multi-Scale Phenomena** — The organism plot uses log scales on both axes to accommodate bacteria (1 micron) to sequoias (100 meters) and generation times from hours to centuries. The scale choice is visible in the axis labels but not discussed as a design decision.

## Notes on Tufte's Design Philosophy in These Chapters

### Chapter 3: The Institutional Critique

Chapter 3 is unusual in Tufte's corpus for being primarily a sociological and institutional critique rather than a design manual. Key themes:

1. **Political Economy of Graphics**: The "doctrine of boring data" serves institutional interests by justifying art department expansion and removing graphics from substantive expertise.

2. **Audience Underestimation**: Repeated emphasis that audiences are more capable than illustrators assume — children can understand relational graphics, college-educated newspaper readers receive pre-adult graphics.

3. **International Comparison**: Japan as counter-example, where statistical culture (Statistics Day holiday, children's graph-drawing contests) produces more sophisticated news graphics.

4. **Quantitative Measure of Sophistication**: The "percentage relational" metric operationalizes sophistication as capacity for causal/explanatory rather than merely descriptive graphics.

### Chapter 4 Opening: The Data-Ink Concept

The beginning of Chapter 4 introduces what will become a central theoretical construct:

1. **Data-Ink as Non-Erasable Core**: The definition emphasizes that data-ink is what cannot be removed without information loss.

2. **Ratio as Design Metric**: The data-ink ratio provides a quantifiable criterion for design decisions.

3. **Historical Demonstration**: Playfair's evolution from cluttered to clean design shows that even the inventor of modern graphics learned this principle through practice.

4. **Continuum of Examples**: From electroencephalogram (100% data-ink) through organism plot (~80-90%) to voter registration chart (very low data-ink), showing the full range.

## Cross-References to Framework Categories

### Evidential Category (Strongest Mapping)

These chapters heavily emphasize the evidential function of graphics. The core argument is that graphics should serve as instruments of evidence and reasoning, not decoration or attention-getting. Key articulations:

- "confront statements about cause and effect with evidence" (relational graphics as tests of claims)
- "Above all else show the data" (evidence primacy)
- "instruments to help people reason about quantitative information" (graphics as cognitive tools)
- Data-ink concept (ink should carry information)

### Relational Category

The sophistication metric explicitly privileges relational graphics as the marker of serious data communication. The explanatory/causal function is what separates journalism from decoration.

### Comparative Category

Implicit in the relational emphasis — relationships are shown through comparison of how Y changes as X changes.

### Instructional Category

Weaker presence, but the "reasoning instrument" framing places graphics in the instructional family — they help audiences learn something about the world.

---

## Potential New Intent Categories Suggested

1. **Epistemic Respect**: Treating audience as capable reasoners deserving sophisticated presentation.

2. **Production Quality**: Meta-level concern with how graphics should be made (three expertises).

3. **Anti-Decoration**: Explicit rejection of ornament that doesn't carry data.

4. **Explanatory vs. Descriptive**: Distinction between graphics that show what happened (time-series) and graphics that show why (relational).
