## Extracted Intents

### 1. Dense Information Display

**Name:** Dense Information Display

**Definition:** Communicate large quantities of data efficiently within limited visual space, enabling comprehensive understanding that cannot be achieved through text or tables alone.

**Category Mapping:** Aggregative (summary presentation of complex data)

**How Tufte Articulates It:**
- "More information is better than less information, especially when the marginal costs of handling and interpreting additional information are low"
- "The simple things belong in tables or in the text; graphics can give a sense of large and complex data sets that cannot be managed in any other way"
- "Maximize data density and the size of the data matrix, within reason"

**Examples Referenced:**
- Sunshine record: ~1,000 numbers per square inch
- Map of French communes: ~9,000 numbers per square inch
- Galaxy map: 110,000 numbers per square inch (cited as "current record")
- Comparison table of data density across publications (Nature, Science, WSJ, etc.)

**Source Type:** Explicit prose

**Limitations from Text-Only:** Cannot assess the visual clarity of high-density examples or verify legibility claims.

### 2. Comparative Juxtaposition (Small Multiples)

**Name:** Comparative Juxtaposition

**Definition:** Enable comparison across a changing variable by presenting multiple instances of the same graphic structure, indexed by changes in another dimension (time, category, condition).

**Category Mapping:** Comparative (showing difference across conditions)

**How Tufte Articulates It:**
- "Small multiples resemble the frames of a movie: a series of graphics, showing the same combination of variables, indexed by changes in another variable"
- "The design remains constant through all the frames, so that attention is devoted entirely to shifts in the data"
- Well-designed small multiples are "inevitably comparative"

**Examples Referenced:**
- Los Angeles air pollution over 23 hours (video frames)
- Melanoma distribution on body (with male/female comparison)
- Sampling error distributions (12 samples of 50 random normal deviates)
- Herring age composition 1908-1913 (tracking 1904 spawn cohort)
- Chromosomes of man, chimpanzee, gorilla, orangutan
- Consumer Reports frequency-of-repair records (1976-1981)

**Source Type:** Explicit prose with extensive examples

**Limitations from Text-Only:** Cannot observe how visual constancy enables comparison or assess the effectiveness of the multivariate display.

### 3. Multivariate Revelation

**Name:** Multivariate Revelation

**Definition:** Simultaneously display relationships among multiple variables, revealing patterns that emerge only when several dimensions are viewed together.

**Category Mapping:** Relational (connection between multiple dimensions)

**How Tufte Articulates It:**
- Small multiples are "deftly multivariate"
- They show "shifts in the relationship between variables as the index variable changes (thereby revealing interaction or multiplicative effects)"

**Examples Referenced:**
- Consumer Reports table mixing manufacturer, car type, year, and trouble spots
- Chromosome comparison across species
- Melanoma study with location, gender, and count

**Source Type:** Explicit prose

**Limitations from Text-Only:** Cannot observe how multiple variables are visually encoded simultaneously.

### 4. Temporal Narrative

**Name:** Temporal Narrative

**Definition:** Tell a story about how data changes over time, revealing progression, trends, or significant events through sequential display.

**Category Mapping:** Temporal (when/sequence)

**How Tufte Articulates It:**
- Small multiples are "often narrative in content, showing shifts in the relationship between variables as the index variable changes"
- Graphics "often have a narrative quality, a story to tell about the data"

**Examples Referenced:**
- Herring catch data showing dominance of 1904 spawning class as it ages through catches 1908-1913
- Los Angeles pollution evolution over 23 hours
- Consumer Reports repair records over model years 1976-1981
- Minard's graphics of military campaigns

**Source Type:** Explicit prose

**Limitations from Text-Only:** Cannot observe how temporal sequence creates narrative flow visually.

### 5. Credibility Through Completeness

**Name:** Credibility Through Completeness

**Definition:** Establish trustworthiness of the presentation by showing comprehensive data rather than selective summaries, allowing the audience to verify claims against full evidence.

**Category Mapping:** Evidential (confidence and provenance)

**How Tufte Articulates It:**
- "Data-rich designs give a context and credibility to statistical evidence"
- "Low-information designs are suspect: what is left out, what is hidden, why are we shown so little?"
- "Summary graphics can emerge from high-information displays, but there is nowhere to go if we begin with a low-information design"

**Examples Referenced:**
- Contrast between high-density scientific graphics and low-density news graphics
- Comparison of Nature (48 numbers/sq in median) vs Pravda (0.2 numbers/sq in)

**Source Type:** Explicit prose

**Limitations from Text-Only:** Cannot compare visual impression of "complete" vs "sparse" presentations.

### 6. Within-View Comparison

**Name:** Within-View Comparison

**Definition:** Enable the eye to make comparisons without requiring memory or page-turning by presenting related data elements within a single visual field.

**Category Mapping:** Comparative (difference assessment)

**How Tufte Articulates It:**
- "High-density graphics help us to compare parts of the data by displaying much information within the view of the eye"
- "We look at one page at a time and the more on the page, the more effective and comparative our eye can be"
- References x-ray analysis advice to "search a reduced image so that the whole display can be perceived on at least one occasion without large eye movement"

**Examples Referenced:**
- Bertin's 17 small-scale graphics on single page
- Consumer Reports frequency-of-repair matrix

**Source Type:** Explicit prose with citation to perceptual research

**Limitations from Text-Only:** Cannot assess visual span or eye movement requirements of examples.

### 7. Spatial Distribution

**Name:** Spatial Distribution

**Definition:** Show how phenomena are distributed across geographic or anatomical space, revealing patterns of concentration, absence, or variation.

**Category Mapping:** Distributional (shape/spread in space)

**How Tufte Articulates It:**
- Implied through examples rather than explicit principle
- Maps described as achieving 100,000-250,000 bits per square inch
- "Perhaps some day statistical graphics will perform as successfully as maps in carrying information"

**Examples Referenced:**
- Map of 30,000 French communes
- Melanoma distribution on human body
- Los Angeles pollution spatial distribution
- Galaxy map with location encoding

**Source Type:** Inferred from examples

**Limitations from Text-Only:** Cannot observe spatial encoding or distribution patterns directly.

### 8. Appropriate Format Selection

**Name:** Appropriate Format Selection

**Definition:** Match the presentation format (sentence, table, graphic, or hybrid) to the nature and volume of data, enabling the most effective comparison and comprehension.

**Category Mapping:** Meta-intent (choosing communication mode)

**How Tufte Articulates It:**
- "The substantive content, extensiveness of labels, and volume and ordering of data all help determine the choice of method for the display of quantitative materials"
- "The conventional sentence is a poor way to show more than two numbers because it prevents comparisons within the data"
- "Tables are clearly the best way to show exact numerical values"
- "Tables are preferable to graphics for many small data sets"

**Examples Referenced:**
- Text-table transformation (prose to aligned percentages)
- "Supertable" for 1976/1980 election voting patterns (410 numbers)
- Critique of pie charts as failing to order numbers along visual dimension

**Source Type:** Explicit prose

**Limitations from Text-Only:** N/A - this is primarily a textual argument.

### 9. Ordered Storytelling

**Name:** Ordered Storytelling

**Definition:** Arrange data elements in a sequence that reveals meaning, rather than arbitrary orderings like alphabetical, so the structure itself communicates.

**Category Mapping:** Temporal / Comparative (meaningful sequence)

**How Tufte Articulates It:**
- "There are nearly always better sequences than alphabetical -- for example, ordering by content or by data values"
- Election supertable: "the rows are ordered so as to tell an ordered story about the elections"

**Examples Referenced:**
- Group percentages reordered from ABC to BCA (by ascending value)
- Election voting table with topical paragraphs and meaningful row order

**Source Type:** Explicit prose

**Limitations from Text-Only:** Can observe the textual argument; ordering is a structural property visible in text.

### 10. Graphical Elegance Through Simplicity

**Name:** Graphical Elegance

**Definition:** Achieve visual quality through simplicity of design combined with complexity of data, avoiding decoration that does not serve information.

**Category Mapping:** Meta-intent (design quality principle)

**How Tufte Articulates It:**
- "Graphical elegance is often found in simplicity of design and complexity of data"
- "For non-data-ink, less is more. For data-ink, less is a bore."
- "Beautiful graphics do not traffic with the trivial"
- Graphics should "avoid content-free decoration, including chartjunk"

**Examples Referenced:**
- Minard's campaign graphics
- Playfair and Marey's work
- Galaxy map as example of modern quality work

**Source Type:** Explicit prose

**Limitations from Text-Only:** Cannot observe visual elegance directly; must rely on Tufte's characterization.

### 11. Meaningful Content

**Name:** Meaningful Content

**Definition:** Graphics should address matters of importance and significance, deriving power from the weight of their subject matter.

**Category Mapping:** Meta-intent (content significance)

**How Tufte Articulates It:**
- "Visually attractive graphics also gather power from content and interpretations beyond the immediate display of some numbers"
- "The best graphics are about the useful and important, about life and death, about the universe"

**Examples Referenced:**
- Minard's depiction of Napoleon's Russian campaign losses
- Galaxy map

**Source Type:** Explicit prose

**Limitations from Text-Only:** N/A - this is an argument about content selection.
