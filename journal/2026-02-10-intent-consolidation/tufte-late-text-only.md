## Identified Communicative Intents

### 1. Multi-Dimensional Temporal-Spatial Narrative

**Definition**: Communicating how multiple variables change across both time and space simultaneously, revealing the interplay between location and temporal dynamics.

**How Tufte articulates it**: The air pollution display shows "the levels of three air pollutants located over a two-dimensional surface (six counties in southern California) at four times during the day." The text emphasizes how the visualization narrates causal sequences: "Nitrogen oxides... are emitted by power plants, refineries, and vehicles. Refineries along the coast and Kaiser Steel's Fontana plant produce the post-midnight peaks."

**Examples referenced**:
- Air pollution display showing nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, and reactive hydrocarbons across Los Angeles counties at four times of day
- Japanese beetle life cycle graphic that "ingeniously mixes space and time on the horizontal axis"

**Framework category mapping**:
- Primary: **Temporal** (when/sequence)
- Secondary: **Distributional** (shape/spread across space)
- Also relates to: **Relational** (showing how variables interact with location and time)

---

### 2. Small Multiple Pattern Recognition

**Definition**: Enabling viewers to perceive patterns across multiple instances by holding graphical design constant while varying only the data, thereby making changes in data salient.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Small multiples are economical: once viewers understand the design of one slice, they have immediate access to the data in all the other slices. Thus, as the eye moves from one slice to the next, the constancy of the design allows the viewer to focus on changes in the data rather than on changes in graphical design."

**Examples referenced**:
- Air pollution display with 12 time-space-pollutant slices
- Phillips curve plots for nine countries showing unemployment and inflation

**Framework category mapping**:
- Primary: **Comparative** (difference)
- Supports: Pattern recognition as a meta-intent that enables comparison across structured repetition

---

### 3. Causal Relationship Assessment

**Definition**: Inviting the viewer to assess whether one variable causes another by presenting their empirical relationship visually, confronting theories with evidence.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "The relational graphic... is the greatest of all graphical designs. It links at least two variables, encouraging and even imploring the viewer to assess the possible causal relationship between the plotted variables. It confronts causal theories that X causes Y with empirical evidence as to the actual relationship between X and Y."

**Examples referenced**:
- Lung cancer death rate vs. cigarette consumption scatterplot (1950/1930 data across countries)
- Thermal conductivity of copper measurements from multiple laboratories

**Framework category mapping**:
- Primary: **Relational** (causation)
- Also: **Evidential** (claim + evidence structure)

---

### 4. Theory-Evidence Confrontation

**Definition**: Presenting empirical data that either confirms or refutes theoretical predictions, making the divergence or alignment visible.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Theory and measured observations diverge in the physical sciences, also." The Phillips curve example shows "the collapse of what was once thought to be an inverse relationship between the variables."

**Examples referenced**:
- Phillips curve plots demonstrating theory collapse
- Thermal conductivity measurements showing "very different answers reported in the published literature"

**Framework category mapping**:
- Primary: **Evidential** (claim + evidence)
- Secondary: **Comparative** (deviation from expected)

---

### 5. Cross-Study Synthesis

**Definition**: Organizing findings from multiple independent studies on a single display to enable comparison of varying results and expose methodological differences.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Note how effectively the graphic organizes a vast amount of data, recording findings of hundreds of studies on a single page and, at the same time, enforcing comparisons of the varying results."

**Examples referenced**:
- Thermal conductivity of copper compilation (connected points from single publications, identified by number)

**Framework category mapping**:
- Primary: **Aggregative** (summary across sources)
- Secondary: **Comparative** (showing variation in results)
- Also: **Evidential** (provenance via identification numbers)

---

### 6. Relational Abstraction

**Definition**: Representing abstract quantitative relationships freed from geographic or temporal coordinates, allowing any variable to be plotted against any other.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "The invention of data graphics required replacing the latitude-longitude coordinates of the map with more abstract measures not based on geographical analogy... Data graphics, because they were relational and not tied to geographic or time coordinates, became relevant to all quantitative inquiry."

Lambert's statement: "We have in general two variable quantities, x, y, which will be collated with one another by observation."

**Examples referenced**:
- Lambert's evaporation rate derivation (graphical calculus)
- Playfair's Statistical Breviary plate with circles, population, and revenue lines

**Framework category mapping**:
- Primary: **Relational** (connection between variables)
- Historical significance: Marks the conceptual breakthrough enabling modern data graphics

---

### 7. Multivariate Comparison

**Definition**: Displaying multiple variables simultaneously for the same units of observation to foster comparisons that would be impossible with single-variable presentations.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Playfair's design is "distinguished by its multivariate data, the use of area to depict quantity, and the pie chart." Playfair: "I have therefore represented these three objects [Size, Population, Revenue] in one view."

The principle: "where forms are not similar, the eye cannot compare them easily nor accurately."

**Examples referenced**:
- Playfair's Statistical Breviary plate with circles for area, vertical lines for population and revenue
- First documented pie chart

**Framework category mapping**:
- Primary: **Comparative** (multi-attribute comparison)
- Secondary: **Aggregative** (combining multiple measures)

---

### 8. Data-as-Mark Representation

**Definition**: Using the actual data objects (photographs, drawings, specimens) as the plotting marks themselves, rather than abstract geometric symbols.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Two relational designs of a different sort--wherein the data points are themselves data. Here the effect of two variables interacting is portrayed by the faces on the plotting field."

**Examples referenced**:
- Lorenz dog faces showing interaction of fear and rage
- White pine seedling photographs showing growth under varying calcium levels

**Framework category mapping**:
- Primary: **Relational** (showing effect of variable interaction)
- Form innovation: Merges data representation with evidence display

---

### 9. High-Density Information Efficiency

**Definition**: Maximizing the amount of meaningful information communicated per unit of display space and viewer time.

**How Tufte articulates it**: "Graphical excellence is that which gives to the viewer the greatest number of ideas in the shortest time with the least ink in the smallest space."

The air pollution display: "Thus 28,800 pollutant readings are shown, except for those masked by peaks."

**Examples referenced**:
- Air pollution display (28,800 readings)
- Thermal conductivity compilation (hundreds of studies on single page)

**Framework category mapping**:
- Meta-principle rather than intent category
- Affects how all intents should be realized

---

### 10. Physical-to-Abstract Transition Justification

**Definition**: Explaining and justifying the validity of representing abstract quantities through physical visual space.

**How Tufte articulates it**: Playfair's argument that charts "correspond to a physical realization of the data" - imagining guineas laid in lines whose lengths represent amounts. "The Charts are exactly this upon a small scale."

Closing quotes acknowledge this concern: "As to the propriety and justness of representing sums of money, and time, by parts of space, tho' very readily agreed to by most men, yet a few seem to apprehend that there may possibly be some deception in it."

**Framework category mapping**:
- Primary: **Explanation** (justifying the representational method)
- Historical: Documents early skepticism and rhetorical responses

## Principles of Graphical Excellence (Summary Statement)

Tufte concludes with five principles that function as meta-intents governing all data graphic design:

1. **Substance + Statistics + Design**: "Graphical excellence is the well-designed presentation of interesting data--a matter of substance, of statistics, and of design."

2. **Clarity-Precision-Efficiency**: "Graphical excellence consists of complex ideas communicated with clarity, precision, and efficiency."

3. **Information Density**: "Graphical excellence is that which gives to the viewer the greatest number of ideas in the shortest time with the least ink in the smallest space."

4. **Multivariate Richness**: "Graphical excellence is nearly always multivariate."

5. **Truthfulness**: "And graphical excellence requires telling the truth about the data."

## Limitations from Text-Only Analysis

### What Could Not Be Extracted

1. **Visual complexity assessment**: Cannot evaluate whether the figures actually achieve the density and clarity Tufte claims. The air pollution display reportedly shows 28,800 readings--the visual organization enabling this cannot be assessed.

2. **Small multiple effectiveness**: The actual visual rhythm and how the eye traverses the repeated panels cannot be experienced. The claim that "constancy of design allows the viewer to focus on changes in data" relies on visual demonstration.

3. **Spatial encoding specifics**: How exactly the three pollutants are mapped to visual variables (color? height? saturation?) is not clear from text.

4. **Pie chart and area encoding**: Playfair's innovations are described but the visual execution cannot be evaluated.

5. **Data-as-mark impact**: The Lorenz dog faces and pine seedling photographs would demonstrate a radically different approach to marks--the emotional and evidential impact requires visual inspection.

6. **Lambert's graphical calculus**: The derivation process (taking tangent slopes, replotting) would be clearer with figures showing the transformation sequence.

7. **Scatterplot interpretation**: The lung cancer correlation and its visual persuasiveness depend on seeing the actual point distribution and the fitted line.

### What Text Preserves Well

1. **Explicit principles**: Tufte's five summary principles are fully captured.
2. **Historical argumentation**: The Lambert/Playfair conceptual breakthrough narrative is complete.
3. **Causal intent claims**: The assertion that scatterplots "implore the viewer to assess causal relationships" is textually explicit.
4. **Quantitative claims**: Numbers of data points, countries, pollutants are preserved.
5. **Source attribution**: Citations enable locating original materials.

### Methodological Note

This text-only extraction demonstrates that Tufte's communicative intents are partially expressible in prose, but his fundamental argument--that visual display achieves things prose cannot--means the text necessarily underrepresents the evidence for his claims. The intents identified here should be validated against the actual figures in a multimodal analysis.
