# 010 Generation Attempt 2

## Prompt Used

```
You are helping draft a research problem statement.

## Context

Throughout human history, complex knowledge has been transmitted through speech. Oral traditions preserved epics, genealogies, laws, and technical knowledge across generations. Rhetoric developed sophisticated frameworks for organizing spoken argument. Radio created new forms for journalism, drama, and documentary. Podcasts continue evolving techniques for conveying complex information to listeners.

A software system called "apparatus" needs to communicate technical information to researchers through speech. The system speaks; the researcher listens. To design this well, we need to understand how spoken communication is structured.

## Your Task

Write a research problem statement exploring how technical content is structured in spoken form. The research should explore:
- What forms and structures exist in oral traditions, rhetoric, radio, and podcasts
- How speakers organize complex information for listeners
- What vocabulary exists for describing the structure of speech
- How listeners parse and comprehend structured spoken content

## Structural Template

Use this structure:

1. **Title** - Short, descriptive
2. **Context** (2-3 paragraphs) - Why this research matters
3. **Research Questions** - Organized into 3-5 categories, 2-4 questions each
4. **Systems to Examine** - Categories of sources to investigate
5. **Approach** - 3-4 phases of research
6. **Working Directory** - Where to store notes (use path: `/work/apparatus/investigations/semantic-presentation/research/010-auditory-forms/`)
7. **Expected Outputs** - What the research should produce
8. **Non-Goals** - What's out of scope

## Tone

Write with directness and genuine inquiry. Examples from similar research documents:

> "Research into the minimal set of structural primitives that should form the core vocabulary..."

> "These forms exist at different levels of complexity... But is this the right decomposition?"

The researcher reading this should feel free to explore and discover. We genuinely don't know what structures exist or how they organize — that's what we're trying to find out.

Output only the research document content (markdown format), nothing else.
```

## Key Changes from Attempt 1

3. **No mention of visual** — didn't say "no screen" or "not translation"
4. **No accessibility framing** — screen readers not mentioned as a system to examine
5. **Historical grounding** — oral tradition as sophisticated system, not primitive precursor
