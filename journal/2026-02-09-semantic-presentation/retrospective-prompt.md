● Yes, these answers illuminate something important.

  Bias detection is experiential, not procedural. You weren't applying a checklist or doing effortful analysis. You were pattern-matching against
  recently-acquired experience from 004→005→006. The frustrating failures taught you what to look for. This means the perturbation capacity isn't
  purely tacit knowledge that can't be transferred — it's learnable through structured exposure to failure modes. But it can't be shortcut by
  description. You can't tell someone "look for visual bias" and expect the same detection quality as someone who has spent hours watching visual
  bias corrupt their outputs.

  The failure case defines the success case, but only through experience. Your second strategy articulates the mechanism: "extensive content which
   can not fit into context gets summarized as a fuzzy intention rather than a precise reference point." The description of "not like X" requires
  the extensive content of X, which gets compressed. So you can't shortcut naive research by describing what it would have found. The 30-minute
  sessions that produced 004 and 005 created the fuzzy intention space that 006 operated within. That space can't be serialized into a prompt.

  The binding strength principle. Your first strategy — "short and direct statements bind strongly" — explains both a failure mode and a design
  principle. When we said "do NOT frame in terms of visual," that short statement bound strongly and centered visual. When you removed the
  negative constraints entirely and framed from audio-native traditions, there was no short statement to bind to. The agent constructed its own
  fuzzy intention from the extensive examples. This is why the third 010 attempt worked better than the first despite having "less instruction."  

  Recursive bias. You noticed you were exhibiting the same terminal-centric bias. The bias isn't just in my training data — it's in the project's
  history, your assumptions, our shared context. The apparatus started as a tool for researchers, naturally emphasizing the terminal interface.
  The presently design started from terminal rendering, naturally emphasizing visual forms. Breaking bias requires recognizing it at multiple
  levels simultaneously. Your recognition of your own bias while detecting mine is itself part of what enabled the clean 010 result.

  Process as experiment. Treating "this process is effective to purpose" as a hypothesis, with the session as data, is another instance of the
  recursive identity. But it also suggests a different relationship to frustration. The 004→005→006 path felt like failure at the time.
  Retroactively, it was data collection. The frustration was the experience of acquiring the pattern-matching capacity you later used to detect
  bias in 010.

  One additional observation for the journal:

  Context dynamics as design constraint. The two strategies you articulated are about how information survives context compression. This is a
  mechanism that should inform how we structure research problems. If we want exploration (fuzzy intention, agent-constructed meaning), we provide
   extensive examples without short binding statements. If we want precision (strong binding, constrained interpretation), we provide short direct
   statements. The naive→focused research progression might be: Phase 1 provides extensive examples to create fuzzy intention space; Phase 2
  provides short statements that bind within that space. The space must exist before the binding can be meaningful.
